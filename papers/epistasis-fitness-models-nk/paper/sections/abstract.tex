How much non-additive structure can simple regressors learn from a few hundred to a few thousand variants? We study this on \emph{simulated} NK landscapes only, at two settings of length and alphabet, $(L,A)=(15,4)$ and $(20,20)$ (so $L$ and $A$ are confounded), with $K\in\{0,1,2,4\}$, comparing reimplemented one-hot ridge, pairwise-epistasis ridge, a small CNN and a small MLP, with held-out Spearman and one greedy design step as endpoints (5 seeds, 440 CPU runs). At $K=0$ additive ridge is near-perfect (Spearman $1.000$). Additive Spearman at $N=1000$ falls to $0.596$ at $K=1$ and $0.310$ at $K=2$ for $(15,4)$, and to $0.110$ at $K=1$ for $(20,20)$. Pairwise ridge recovers $K=1$ at $(15,4)$ ($0.998$) but gains little at $(20,20)$ ($0.138$ versus $0.110$); the networks never exceed it at $N=1000$, and on the $(15,4)$, $K=2$ task they remain below it at $N=4000$ ($0.616$ and $0.628$ versus $0.669$). At $K=4$, and at $(20,20)$ for $K\ge2$, every model's mean Spearman at $N=1000$ is at most $0.080$. The pre-registered design criterion, a positive rank correlation between the Spearman gain and the design-hit gain of pairwise over additive ridge, is met ($0.52$ over 40 task--seed units), but the evidence is weak: one task cell carries it ($0.24$ without that cell), and with 10 proposals and 5 seeds the design hit rates at $K\ge2$ are not reliably distinguishable from random proposals. Results concern this simulator only.
