Sequence-to-fitness regression is the workhorse of low-data protein engineering: a few hundred to a few thousand measured variants are used to fit a model, which then ranks unseen variants or proposes new ones. Benchmarks such as FLIP and ProteinGym \cite{dallago2021flip,notin2023proteingym} compare such predictors on experimental data, and linear regression on one-hot site features, augmented with an evolutionary density feature, has been reported to be competitive with more elaborate models when labels are scarce \cite{hsu2022learning}. A recurring question is how much of a model's remaining error is due to epistasis, i.e.\ non-additive interactions between sites. On experimental landscapes such as GB1 \cite{wu2016adaptation} or GFP \cite{sarkisyan2016local} the order and strength of epistasis are not known in advance, so this question is hard to answer cleanly.

We therefore use \emph{simulated} landscapes, where it is controlled. Kauffman and Weinberger's NK model \cite{kauffman1989nk} makes the contribution of each site depend on $K$ other sites, so that $K=0$ is exactly additive and larger $K$ is more rugged. \textbf{All data in this paper are simulated; no real protein is modelled and nothing here is evidence about real fitness landscapes.} We ask how four simple regressors---an additive one-hot ridge model, a pairwise-epistasis ridge model, a small CNN and a small MLP---behave as $K$, the $(L,A)$ setting (length and alphabet size, varied together) and training size $N$ vary, using two endpoints: Spearman correlation on held-out random variants, and the success of one greedy design step that proposes single mutants of the best measured sequence.

Contributions (scale: a small CPU study, 5 seeds, all baselines reimplemented): (i) a hashed-table NK simulator that handles $A=20$, $K=4$ without storing tables; (ii) an exact kernel form of pairwise ridge regression, so that it is cheap even with tens of thousands of pair features; (iii) a pre-registered set of five hypotheses (H1--H5) checked cell by cell, including regimes where every model fails.
