\paragraph{NK landscapes.} The NK model \cite{kauffman1989nk} is the standard tunable-ruggedness landscape; its adaptive walks under different interaction schemes have been analysed in \cite{nowak2015analysis}, and its free energy in the regime where $K/N$ is fixed with spin-glass methods in \cite{chen2025fitness}. We use the common random-neighbourhood scheme; those works study landscape properties (walk lengths, free energy), whereas we study supervised learnability, which they do not address.

\paragraph{Epistasis in data.} Experimental landscapes such as GB1 \cite{wu2016adaptation} and GFP \cite{sarkisyan2016local} report epistatic interactions between mutations. A review of global epistasis \cite{diazcolunga2022global} describes patterns in which the fitness effect of a mutation is well predicted by the fitness of its genetic background, and notes that such patterns may emerge from microscopic interactions between mutations or from nonlinearities of the landscape. Our simulator applies no global nonlinearity, and we did not test whether global-epistasis patterns emerge in its landscapes, so our results do not speak to that question.

\paragraph{Supervised fitness prediction and design.} FLIP \cite{dallago2021flip} and ProteinGym \cite{notin2023proteingym} are widely used benchmarks on real assays; \cite{hsu2022learning} reports that linear regression on one-hot site features combined with an evolutionary density feature is competitive with more elaborate methods when labels are scarce (our additive baseline is the plain one-hot ridge model, without the density feature, which has no counterpart in a simulated landscape), and \cite{biswas2020low} studies low-N engineering with learned representations. FLEXS/AdaLead \cite{sinai2020adalead} provides simulated landscapes and greedy sequence-design algorithms; our design step is a much simpler single greedy proposal step from the best measured sequence, not AdaLead. We do not use pretrained language models.
