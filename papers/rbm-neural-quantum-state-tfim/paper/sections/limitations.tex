\textbf{Scale.} One model, $N\le12$, 5 seeds, \rhval{const/iterations} iterations, 256 samples: nothing here extrapolates to large systems, 2D or frustrated models. \textbf{Statistics.} The paired tests are uncorrected and have little power. \textbf{Post-hoc protocol.} The two-start selection was introduced after registration and after an audit of single-start results; it uses the exactly evaluated energy, which a large-$N$ study would not have. \textbf{Tuning.} Rates were tuned from start S only. Five of ten selected rates lie on a grid edge (MF with both optimisers and RBM $\alpha{=}2,4$ with SR at the top, Jastrow with SR at the bottom), so those systems might improve with a wider grid. SGD used no momentum or Adam (an adaptive optimiser could shrink the SR--SGD gap), and SR was not retuned jointly with the shift. \textbf{Baselines.} All are our own reimplementations, not NetKet or published code, without translation symmetry. For $\Gamma\le0.5$ the Jastrow VMC results remain above the Jastrow L-BFGS values (Table~\ref{tab:energy}), so that baseline is still under-optimised there. \textbf{Reference.} The L-BFGS reference uses one seed, covers MF, Jastrow and RBM $\alpha{=}2$ only, and did not converge for the RBM. \textbf{Observables.} Only $\langle|M_z|\rangle$ and fidelity at fixed $N$ are reported ($\langle\sigma^x\rangle$ errors are logged but not tabulated), with no finite-size scaling or correlation functions. A full study would add NetKet-grade baselines, symmetric ansaetze, MinSR or Adam, larger $N$, more seeds, and tuning that covers both starts.
