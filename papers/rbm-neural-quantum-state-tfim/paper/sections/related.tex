\textbf{Neural quantum states.} The RBM wavefunction and its SR optimisation were introduced in~\cite{carleo2016solving}. Representational aspects of Boltzmann machines and deeper variants were studied in~\cite{gao2017efficient}, and RBMs combined with pair-product states were used for strongly correlated fermions in~\cite{nomura2017restricted}. Autoregressive models allow exact sampling~\cite{sharir2019deep,hibatallah2020recurrent}, and symmetric convolutional networks were applied to the frustrated $J_1$--$J_2$ model~\cite{choo2019study}. Our RBM is the original shallow form, with real weights and no symmetries.

\textbf{Optimisation.} SR~\cite{sorella1998green} preconditions the energy gradient with the covariance matrix of the log-derivatives of the wavefunction; the resulting update has the form of a natural-gradient step~\cite{amari1998natural}. MinSR~\cite{chen2023empowering} makes this tractable for networks with many parameters; our networks have at most a few hundred parameters, so dense SR is used.

\textbf{Classical variational baselines.} Product (mean-field) states and two-body Jastrow wavefunctions~\cite{jastrow1955many} are two simple classical variational families; we use them as baselines. Both are reimplemented here and trained with the same two optimisers and the same budget as the RBMs.

\textbf{Benchmarks and tools.} NetKet~\cite{vicentini2021netket} provides production implementations of RBM, SR and ED; the variational benchmark collection of~\cite{wu2023variational} reports energies and variances for larger systems. We used neither, so our absolute numbers are not comparable with theirs. The TFIM chain is exactly solvable by a mapping to free fermions~\cite{pfeuty1970one}; we use ED as the reference instead because it gives the ground-state vector, from which the fidelity and $\langle|M_z|\rangle$ follow directly.
