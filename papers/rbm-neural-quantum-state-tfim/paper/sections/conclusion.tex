In a small VMC study of the periodic TFIM at $N=8$--$12$ with two starts per run, an $\alpha{=}2$ RBM trained by SR gave a lower mean energy error than Jastrow and mean-field states with SR, and than the same RBM with SGD, in all 13 tasks. The margin over Jastrow is large and backed by the sampling-free reference only for $\Gamma\ge0.75$; for $\Gamma\le0.5$ it is at most a factor of \rhval{cmp/selected/jastrow-sr/tfim_n8_g0.50/rel_energy_error/inv_ratio:2} and, in three of four tasks, reflects optimisation under this budget. At the critical field $\alpha{=}4$ was better than the method of record. The results depend on the start (Jastrow and mean-field fail from the symmetric start at small fields, and RBM-SR diverged only from it), on the SR shift and on the sample budget, and low-energy states in the ordered phase are often symmetry-broken. These findings hold for this testbed and tuning rule; they should be re-tested with an adaptive optimiser, per-start tuning, symmetric ansaetze and larger systems.
