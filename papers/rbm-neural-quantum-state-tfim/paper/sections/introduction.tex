Neural quantum states (NQS) represent a many-body wavefunction by a neural network and optimise it variationally. Carleo and Troyer introduced the restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM) as a variational ansatz, optimised with Monte Carlo sampling and stochastic reconfiguration (SR), and tested it on the transverse-field Ising and Heisenberg models~\cite{carleo2016solving}. Since then the idea has spread to autoregressive~\cite{sharir2019deep,hibatallah2020recurrent} and convolutional~\cite{choo2019study} wavefunctions, to better optimisers for large networks~\cite{chen2023empowering}, and to standard software~\cite{vicentini2021netket} and benchmark collections~\cite{wu2023variational}.

At the smallest scales, where exact diagonalisation (ED) is cheap, the questions are different from those at scale and are rarely isolated in one table: how much of an RBM's accuracy comes from the ansatz and how much from the optimiser, how it compares with the two simplest classical variational families under the \emph{same} optimiser and sample budget, and whether an accurate energy implies an accurate order parameter across a quantum phase transition. The one-dimensional transverse-field Ising model (TFIM)~\cite{pfeuty1970one} is a good test bed: it has a critical point at $\Gamma=J$, a nodeless ground state (so real positive amplitudes suffice), and for $N\le 12$ the full Hilbert space has at most $2^{12}=$ \rhval{const/hilbert_dim_max} states.

We study this on a small numpy testbed. The ansaetze are a mean-field (product) state, a two-body Jastrow state~\cite{jastrow1955many}, and RBMs with $\alpha=1,2,4$ hidden units per spin; the optimisers are plain SGD and SR~\cite{sorella1998green}. We pre-registered five hypotheses (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}), ran \rhval{const/main_runs} main runs (two starts), and report which were supported. Contributions are modest and empirical: (i) an optimiser-by-ansatz grid with exactly evaluated errors in energy, magnetisation and fidelity; (ii) a start ablation and a sampling-free reference optimisation that separate ansatz error from optimisation error; (iii) an account of failures (divergence of SR, stuck symmetric starts, symmetry-broken low-energy states) and protocol revisions. We make no claim of a new method.
