# Landscape

Neural quantum states (NQS) were introduced by Carleo and Troyer [carleo2016solving], who used a restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM) log-amplitude optimised by variational Monte Carlo (VMC) with stochastic reconfiguration (SR) [sorella1998green] and reported accurate ground states of the 1D transverse-field Ising model (TFIM, solved exactly in [pfeuty1970one]) and the Heisenberg chain. Related early work shows that RBMs can be represented compactly [gao2017efficient] and combined with pair-product states for strongly correlated systems [nomura2017restricted]. Later work moved to autoregressive [sharir2019deep] and recurrent [hibatallah2020recurrent] ansaetze with exact sampling, to symmetric convolutional networks for frustrated models [choo2019study], and to better optimisers for large networks [chen2023empowering]. NetKet 3 [vicentini2021netket] is the standard toolbox; variational benchmarks are collected in [wu2023variational].

Closest work: the original RBM paper (TFIM and Heisenberg, 1D and 2D, N up to 80+ spins). What is less often shown at tiny scale in one controlled table: (i) an optimiser-by-ansatz factorial on the same sample budget, (ii) comparison against the two simplest classical variational families (mean-field, two-body Jastrow) with the same optimiser, (iii) errors of an order parameter, not only energy, across the critical point.
Gap: a controlled, fully reproducible numpy study at N = 8-12 where every variational state can be evaluated exactly by enumeration, separating optimiser effects from ansatz expressivity.
