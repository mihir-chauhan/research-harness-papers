\paragraph{Neural-guided theorem proving.} DeepMath~\cite{alemi2016deepmath} showed that neural networks can select premises for a first-order prover, and Loos et al.~\cite{loos2017deep} used deep networks to guide clause selection in a saturation prover. Kaliszyk et al.~\cite{kaliszyk2018reinforcement} combined Monte-Carlo guidance with learned policy and value estimates for connection-tableau proving. GPT-f~\cite{polu2020generative} introduced autoregressive transformer tactic generation with best-first search for Metamath; HyperTree Proof Search~\cite{lample2022hypertree} adds an MCTS-style search over hypergraph proof states with online training, and BFS-Prover~\cite{xin2025bfs} revisits plain best-first tree search with large language models in Lean, arguing that a simple search with a strong policy can be competitive. LeanDojo~\cite{yang2023leandojo} provides an open benchmark and retrieval-augmented tactic generation, and Aygün et al.~\cite{aygn2021proving} learn provers by incremental training with hindsight experience replay. All of these operate at a scale where the contribution of the learned policy is entangled with pretraining, retrieval and search design; the present study keeps the architecture of the pipeline (policy $\rightarrow$ cost $\rightarrow$ best-first search) but shrinks everything to a regime in which every component can be ablated.

\paragraph{Reasoning benchmarks and generalisation.} Synthetic tasks have long been used to probe whether neural sequence models reason: the mathematics dataset of Saxton et al.~\cite{saxton2019analysing} reports weak extrapolation, Clark et al.~\cite{clark2020transformers} train transformers to answer rule-based deduction questions, and Anil et al.~\cite{anil2022exploring} study length generalisation of language models on reasoning tasks. Our test bins are larger than training formulas in the same sense, but the policy's output is used inside a search rather than as a final answer. The transformer follows the standard architecture~\cite{vaswani2017attention}; we use fixed sinusoidal positions and test learned positions as an ablation.

\paragraph{Gap.} To our knowledge, no prior work we examined reports, in one controlled calculus with computable optimal proofs, a tuned hand-written heuristic as a baseline, a learned policy, and a decomposition of the policy's failures into ranking quality versus search-cost design; we did not conduct an exhaustive survey and state this only as the motivation for the study.
