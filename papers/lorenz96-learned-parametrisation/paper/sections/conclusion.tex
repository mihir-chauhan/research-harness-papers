In this small two-scale Lorenz-96 study, all three learned closures extended the forecast valid time well beyond no closure and none diverged. The deterministic polynomial and MLP with a local input were not distinguished by the Welch tests on valid time and spectral error at 5 seeds, the AR(1) closure had the best climate at $c{=}10$ but a shorter valid time, and at $c{=}4$ the climate metrics did not separate the learned closures from the noise floor. In the three-seed ablations, a better offline fit and forecast skill did not come with a better climate (degree 2 against degree 1), the AR(1) closure lost its climate advantage at the two shifted forcings (its noise amplitude, fitted at $F{=}20$, may not transfer), and giving the MLP neighbouring inputs may matter more than the choice of function class; these are exploratory results in an idealised toy system.
