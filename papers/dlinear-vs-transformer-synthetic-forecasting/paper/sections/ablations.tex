\textbf{Design ablation (H5 and normalisation; 400 steps).} Table~\ref{tab:abl} removes the decomposition (DLinear only) or the instance normalisation (each learned model) at $H=96$; ``Full'' is the main-grid row. \emph{H5 is supported}: a single linear map without decomposition changes DLinear's MSE by $-0.2\%$ (trend), $+0.2\%$ (regime) and $+1.9\%$ (etth1), all within 5\%. Our trends are mild and the window normalisation already removes the level, so this does not show the decomposition is useless in general.

Instance normalisation matters more, and not in one direction. On the trend task removing it raises the mean MSE of DLinear by $+66.5\%$, of PatchTST by $+218\%$ and of the GRU to 5.646, but all three means are driven by one dataset (seed 1, whose trend MSE is several times that of the other two seeds). The median per-seed change is $+8.8\%$ for DLinear, $+134\%$ for PatchTST and $+805\%$ for the GRU: the two nonlinear models degrade strongly on a typical seed, DLinear only mildly. On regime, in contrast, removing the normalisation \emph{lowers} MSE for all three models ($-5.6\%$ DLinear, $-1.9\%$ PatchTST, $-3.6\%$ GRU; medians $-5.4\%$, $-1.2\%$, $-3.5\%$), and on etth1 it hurts the GRU ($+10.1\%$) and changes the others by less than 3\%. The regime result is exploratory (not a registered hypothesis); window-statistics normalisation may hurt when the signal scale changes between regimes, but we did not test that.
