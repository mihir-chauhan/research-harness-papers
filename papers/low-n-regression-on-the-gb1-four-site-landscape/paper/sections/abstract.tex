Supervised models of protein fitness are usually compared with a few hundred to thousands of labels, yet simple baselines are often hard to beat.
We reimplement four regressors on the complete GB1 four-site landscape (\rhval{const/n_variants} variants): one-hot ridge, one-hot ridge with all pairwise site-by-site features, a Gaussian process with a Hamming-distance kernel, and a small CNN.
Each is trained on $N\in\{48,96,384,2000\}$ variants drawn either uniformly at random or only from wild-type, single and double mutants, and scored on all remaining variants (Spearman and top-100 recall; \rhval{const/n_seeds} training draws per cell).
Pairwise epistasis features gave no detectable gain at any random-training size (Spearman \rhval{main/one-hot-+-pairwise-ridge/rand_2000/spearman/mean:3} versus \rhval{main/one-hot-ridge-reimplemented/rand_2000/spearman/mean:3} for the additive model at $N{=}2000$, paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/one-hot-ridge-reimplemented/rand_2000/spearman/paired_p}$) and badly hurt when training was restricted to single and double mutants ($N{=}2000$: \rhval{main/one-hot-+-pairwise-ridge/dbl_2000/spearman/mean:3} versus \rhval{main/one-hot-ridge-reimplemented/dbl_2000/spearman/mean:3}).
The CNN had the highest mean Spearman at $N\ge 96$ (\rhval{main/cnn-reimplemented/rand_384/spearman/mean:3} at $N{=}384$), but a log transform of the target given to every model closed this gap at $N\le384$ and narrowed it at $N{=}2000$, which is consistent with part of the CNN's edge being a learned output nonlinearity rather than epistasis (the CNN's configuration was tuned on a draw overlapping the test pools, so its lead is an upper estimate).
Mean top-100 recall peaked at \rhval{main/cnn-reimplemented/rand_2000/top100_recall/mean:3} (CNN, $N{=}2000$), and was at most \rhval{main/gp-hamming-kernel-reimplemented/rand_384/top100_recall/mean:3} for any model at $N\le 384$.
With five draws per cell and one landscape, small differences are not resolved; the study is small and does not rank methods in general.
