In a small, exactly-known synthetic setting, training-set size helped on clean labels (H1) and object-level class flips hurt far more than boundary jitter at the same nominal level (H2), with the damage concentrated in the square and triangle classes. The hoped-for dilution of flip noise by more data (H3) did not appear, and none of the pixel-wise robust losses we reimplemented was demonstrably better than plain cross-entropy (H4); the one that came closest, GCE, did not reach significance with four seeds, and our own band-ignore loss was worse than CE where it was meant to help. These conclusions are limited to this architecture, budget and noise model, and to four (or three) seeds per cell; longer-schedule, validation-selected and real-data follow-ups are needed before they would justify advice to practitioners.
