Dense prediction needs dense labels, and dense labels are expensive and imperfect: annotators trace object outlines a few pixels off, and crowd-sourced or semi-automatic pipelines occasionally assign a whole region to the wrong class. Practitioners therefore face two coupled questions: how many (noisy) annotated images are needed, and does the kind of noise matter? Work on label noise has mostly studied image classification~\cite{zhang2016understanding,arpit2017closer,rolnick2017deep,song2020learning}, where a network first fits the clean pattern and only later memorises wrong labels, and where very large datasets can average out even massive uniform noise. Segmentation differs: a label error is a spatially structured set of pixels, boundary errors touch only a thin band around each object, and the effective number of training ``samples'' is the number of pixels, not of images.

We run a controlled, small-scale study of this interaction on a problem in which the ground truth is known exactly. We generate $64\times64$ images of overlapping circles, squares and triangles on textured backgrounds, corrupt only the training masks with two noise models (boundary jitter and whole-object class flips, each at a nominal level of up to 30\%), vary the training-set size from 100 to 2\,000 images, and train a small U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015u} on a CPU. We measure per-class IoU against clean test masks. Besides plain cross-entropy (CE) we reimplement two noise-robust losses from the classification literature, generalized cross-entropy (GCE)~\cite{zhang2018generalized} and symmetric cross-entropy (SCE)~\cite{wang2019symmetric}, and add a simple band-ignore CE that drops the pixels near annotated edges.

We register four hypotheses beforehand (data size helps; object flips hurt more than boundary jitter; noise hurts more with little data; robust losses help) and report each verdict with the test we committed to, including where the evidence is thin. Contributions: (i) a reproducible synthetic benchmark and noise generators; (ii) a size $\times$ noise-type $\times$ noise-level grid with 3--4 seeds per cell and per-class IoU; (iii) a head-to-head of CE, GCE, SCE and band-ignore CE under identical budgets; (iv) an honest account of what a CPU-scale study of this kind can and cannot establish.
