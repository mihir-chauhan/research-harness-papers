\textbf{H1 (SP gap): supported.} SP reaches self-play of at least $\rhval{main/self-play/signal/self_play/mean}$ on every task (\rhval{main/self-play/lever/self_play/mean} lever, \rhval{main/self-play/safe/self_play/mean} safe, \rhval{main/self-play/signal/self_play/mean} signal) but cross-play is $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play/mean:3}$, $\rhval{main/self-play/safe/cross_play/mean:3}$ and $\rhval{main/self-play/signal/cross_play/mean:3}$, a gap of $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/gap/mean:3}$, $\rhval{main/self-play/safe/gap/mean:3}$ and $\rhval{main/self-play/signal/gap/mean:3}$; the worst pair scores at most \rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play_min/max} (lever), \rhval{main/self-play/safe/cross_play_min/max} (safe) and \rhval{main/self-play/signal/cross_play_min/max} (signal) in any seed.

\textbf{H2 (OP beats SP on every task): mixed.} On the safe game (\rhval{main/other-play/safe/cross_play/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/self-play/safe/cross_play/mean:3}, $p=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/safe/cross_play/welch_p}$, $d=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/safe/cross_play/cohen_d:1}$) and the signal game (\rhval{main/other-play/signal/cross_play/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/self-play/signal/cross_play/mean:3}, $p=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/signal/cross_play/welch_p}$) OP is clearly higher. On the lever game, the headline case, OP cross-play is $\rhval{main/other-play/lever/cross_play/mean:3}\pm\rhval{main/other-play/lever/cross_play/std:3}$ against $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play/mean:3}\pm\rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play/std:3}$ for SP; Welch $p=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/lever/cross_play/welch_p:3}$, $d=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/lever/cross_play/cohen_d:1}$. We call this \emph{inconclusive} at five seeds: the direction agrees but the test does not clear $0.05$, and in absolute terms OP is far from the achievable $0.9$. The reason is visible in the self-play column: OP self-play is only $\rhval{main/other-play/lever/self_play/mean:3}$ and only a fraction $\rhval{main/other-play/lever/frac_special/mean:2}$ of OP agents end on the distinct lever (SP: $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/frac_special/mean:2}$); the remainder collapse onto an arbitrary symmetric lever, which OP penalises; a post-hoc check explains this as a basin of attraction: the exact OP objective $0.9p^2+(1-p)^2/9$ in the probability $p$ of the distinct lever pushes $p$ away from lever 9 when $p<0.11$, and the fraction of agents initialised above that threshold ($\rhval{init_check/other-play@init_std=1/lever/frac_init_above/mean:2}$ at sd 1, Table~\ref{tab:init}) is close to the final fraction on the distinct lever ($\rhval{main/other-play/lever/frac_special/mean:2}$). OP therefore does not reliably find the unique equilibrium with tabular REINFORCE (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\textbf{Where OP ``succeeds'' by giving something up.} OP closes the gap on the safe game (cross-play $\rhval{main/other-play/safe/cross_play/mean:3}$ = self-play), but only by choosing the safe action, i.e.\ it coordinates by avoiding the symmetric levers (payoff $1$ in self-play) altogether. In the signal game OP's $\rhval{main/other-play/signal/cross_play/mean:3}$ equals the prior probability of the most likely card: with fully symmetric messages the only relabelling-invariant policy is the pooling one, in which the receiver always guesses card 0 and no information is conveyed. OP thus eliminates the gap by removing communication ($\rhval{main/other-play/signal/self_play/mean:2}$ self-play vs.\ $\rhval{main/self-play/signal/self_play/mean:2}$ for SP); it is better in cross-play but worse than SP in self-play and worse than any symmetry-broken protocol that the partner could in principle learn.

\textbf{H3 (population between SP and OP, grows with $K$): partly supported, partly refuted.} The $K=8$ population has higher cross-play than SP on the safe ($\rhval{main/population-fcp-style/safe/cross_play/mean:3}$ vs \rhval{main/self-play/safe/cross_play/mean:3}) and signal ($\rhval{main/population-fcp-style/signal/cross_play/mean:3}$ vs \rhval{main/self-play/signal/cross_play/mean:3}) games, below OP in both (OP vs population: $p=\rhval{cmp/main/population-fcp-style/safe/cross_play/welch_p:2}$ safe, so only suggestive there; $p=\rhval{cmp/main/population-fcp-style/signal/cross_play/welch_p}$ signal), consistent with the predicted ordering. On the lever game it is indistinguishable from SP ($\rhval{main/population-fcp-style/lever/cross_play/mean:3}$ vs $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play/mean:3}$) and below OP ($p=\rhval{cmp/main/population-fcp-style/lever/cross_play/welch_p:3}$). Cross-play grows with $K$ on safe and signal but not on the lever game (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}, Table~\ref{tab:pop}).
