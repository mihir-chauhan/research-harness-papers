\textbf{H1 (SP gap): supported.} SP reaches self-play of at least $0.97$ on every task (0.991 lever, 1.000 safe, 0.978 signal) but cross-play is $0.084$, $0.268$ and $0.259$, a gap of $0.907$, $0.731$ and $0.718$; the worst pair scores below $10^{-3}$ everywhere.

\textbf{H2 (OP beats SP on every task): mixed.} On the safe game (0.500 vs 0.268, $p=5\times10^{-4}$, $d=6.4$) and the signal game (0.400 vs 0.259, $p<10^{-4}$) OP is clearly higher. On the lever game, the headline case, OP cross-play is $0.153\pm0.057$ against $0.084\pm0.018$ for SP; Welch $p=0.053$, $d=1.6$. We call this \emph{inconclusive} at five seeds: the direction agrees but the test does not clear $0.05$, and in absolute terms OP is far from the achievable $0.9$. The reason is visible in the self-play column: OP self-play is only $0.727$ and only a fraction $0.35$ of OP agents end on the distinct lever (SP: $0.09$); the remainder collapse onto an arbitrary symmetric lever, which OP penalises; a post-hoc check explains this as a basin of attraction: the exact OP objective $0.9p^2+(1-p)^2/9$ in the probability $p$ of the distinct lever pushes $p$ away from lever 9 when $p<0.11$, and the fraction of agents initialised above that threshold ($0.33$ at sd 1, Table~\ref{tab:init}) is close to the final fraction on the distinct lever ($0.35$). OP therefore does not reliably find the unique equilibrium with tabular REINFORCE (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\textbf{Where OP ``succeeds'' by giving something up.} OP closes the gap on the safe game (cross-play $0.500$ = self-play), but only by choosing the safe action, i.e.\ it coordinates by avoiding the symmetric levers (payoff $1$ in self-play) altogether. In the signal game OP's $0.400$ equals the prior probability of the most likely card: with fully symmetric messages the only relabelling-invariant policy is the pooling one, in which the receiver always guesses card 0 and no information is conveyed. OP thus eliminates the gap by removing communication ($0.40$ self-play vs.\ $0.98$ for SP); it is better in cross-play but worse than SP in self-play and worse than any symmetry-broken protocol that the partner could in principle learn.

\textbf{H3 (population between SP and OP, grows with $K$): partly supported, partly refuted.} The $K=8$ population has higher cross-play than SP on the safe ($0.487$ vs 0.268) and signal ($0.337$ vs 0.259) games, below OP in both (OP vs population: $p=0.08$ safe, so only suggestive there; $p=1\times10^{-5}$ signal), consistent with the predicted ordering. On the lever game it is indistinguishable from SP ($0.088$ vs $0.084$) and below OP ($p=0.063$). Cross-play grows with $K$ on safe and signal but not on the lever game (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}, Table~\ref{tab:pop}).

\textbf{Cost.} Runtimes were $\approx2$\,s (SP), $\approx2.5$\,s (OP) and $\approx9$\,s (population) per run of 20 agents; the population method also uses $K{+}1$ trainings per evaluated agent.
