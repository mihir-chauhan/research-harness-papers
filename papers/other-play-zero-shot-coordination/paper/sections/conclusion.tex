In three small symmetric games with exactly evaluated payoffs, self-play agents trained independently coordinate poorly (cross-play $\rhval{main/self-play/lever/cross_play/mean:2}$--$\rhval{main/self-play/safe/cross_play/mean:2}$ against self-play $\ge\rhval{main/self-play/signal/self_play/mean}$). Other-play closes the gap (cross-play equals its self-play) on the safe and signalling games, but by selecting the safe action and abandoning communication, and its improvement in the lever game is small and not clearly distinguishable from noise at five seeds (it finds the distinct lever for about a third of agents, and its cross-play tends to fall as the initial logit scale grows, though a difference is not established at five seeds). A reimplemented population method lies between SP and OP on safe and signal and grows with population size, but does nothing on the lever game. These conclusions are limited to tabular agents, five seeds and our game definitions.
