\textbf{Scale.} Three tiny games, tabular policies, REINFORCE with a fixed optimiser and 20 agents per run; the lever, safe and signal games are our own parameterisations (e.g.\ prior $(0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1)$, lever payoffs $1/0.9$), so absolute values are not comparable with published numbers. Findings need not transfer to deep networks or to Hanabi.

\textbf{Statistics.} Five seeds per cell; the key lever-game OP result has $p=\rhval{cmp/main/self-play/lever/cross_play/welch_p:3}$ and OP-full vs OP has $p=\rhval{cmp/abl_symmetry/op-full-wrong-group/lever/cross_play/welch_p:2}$, so both are inconclusive. Tests assume independent seeds; agents within a seed share a generator and the 380 pair payoffs within a seed are not independent, so we use seed means as units. No multiplicity correction.

\textbf{Baselines.} Population (FCP-style) is a minimal two-stage reimplementation with no checkpoints, diversity bonus, or human data, and uses more compute than SP/OP. We did not run off-belief learning, K-level reasoning, MEP or Any-Play. Hyperparameters were not tuned per system, which may disadvantage whichever method is most sensitive (OP's lever result varies with init scale, though a difference is not established at five seeds).

\textbf{Metric.} Cross-play only measures coordination among agents from the same algorithm (intra-algorithm cross-play), not with humans or other methods, and it can be maximised by trivial, uninformative policies (safe action, pooling signal), as we observe; the metric does not reward getting the self-play payoff back.

\textbf{Process.} The sweep ranges were set after a small unlogged OP lever smoke test on a separate seed; the summed run time of the study was above the nominal 30-minute budget; duplicated registry entries from an overlapping interrupted driver were removed (values were identical). A full study would add more seeds, a tuned OP, initialisation or entropy/exploration variants, and deep policies.
