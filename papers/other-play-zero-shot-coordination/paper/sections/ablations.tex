\textbf{H4: symmetry group (Table~\ref{tab:sym}). Inconclusive (direction agrees) on the lever game, not testable on safe.} Symmetrising over all levers (OP-full), which wrongly includes the distinct lever, yields cross-play $0.108\pm0.017$ against $0.153\pm0.057$ for correct OP ($p=0.16$, not distinguishable at 5 seeds) and removes the distinct-lever solution entirely (frac.\ special $0$ vs $0.35$); it is still above SP ($0.084$) in the mean. So the correct group helps in direction, but five seeds cannot establish it. On the safe game OP and OP-full are equal ($0.500$ each): both choose the safe action, so the safe game cannot separate the two groups; the nominal $p$ of $6\times10^{-6}$ for a difference of $-2\times10^{-5}$ is a numerical artefact of near-zero variance, not an effect.

\textbf{Population size.} On safe, cross-play rises from $0.268$ ($K=1$, equal to training a best response to a single SP agent) to $0.500$ by $K=32$; on signal it rises from $0.255$ to $0.359$ and saturates below OP's $0.400$. On the lever game the sweep is flat within noise ($0.084$--$0.108$) at all $K$: SP partners that pick any of nine equivalent levers rarely include the distinct lever, so the best response cannot generalise to partners from another population. The remaining standard deviations are not shown in Table~\ref{tab:pop}; Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps} includes them.

\textbf{Initialisation scale.} SP cross-play is insensitive to the scale of the initial logits ($0.082$--$0.093$). OP's cross-play tends to fall from $0.179\pm0.076$ (sd 0.5) to $0.104\pm0.036$ (sd 4) while its self-play rises from $0.712$ to $0.840$, but the cross-play difference is within one standard deviation (Welch $p=0.096$ for sd 0.5 vs 4, $0.155$ for sd 1 vs 4; Table~\ref{tab:init}) and is not significant at 5 seeds. The more robust signal is the fraction of OP agents ending on the distinct lever, which falls from $0.35$ (sd 1) to $0.18$ (sd 4). This matches the basin-of-attraction reading: the fraction of agents whose initial probability of lever 9 exceeds $0.11$ is $0.33$ (sd 1) and $0.17$ (sd 4), close to the final fractions (per-seed values are in the \texttt{init\_check} runs). Larger random logits put fewer agents inside the basin; at sd 4 OP is close to SP in cross-play. We report sd 1 as pre-specified and do not claim a tested sensitivity.

\textbf{Training length.} Training OP for ten times longer (15000 updates) raises OP self-play from $0.727$ to $0.941$ but leaves cross-play unchanged ($0.153\to0.151$), and the fraction of agents on the distinct lever stays at $0.350$ across all lengths (500 to 15000 updates). The agents that do not reach lever 9 appear to settle into a deterministic symmetric convention (self-play approaching 1) and stay there; this is our reading of the numbers. The constant fraction is largely by construction: the four lengths are prefixes of one trajectory per seed, so this sweep is one set of trajectories observed at four lengths, not independent replications. Since the basin explanation above is determined at initialisation, longer training cannot help; exact (expected) gradients would not either. Initialisation, entropy regularisation or exploration changes are the natural next step; we did not test them and do not claim OP cannot solve the lever game.
