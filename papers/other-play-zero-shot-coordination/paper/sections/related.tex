\textbf{Other-play and the lever game.} Hu et al.~\cite{hu2020other} introduced OP and the lever game (several equivalent levers and one distinct lever, where SP convention-making is arbitrary and OP prefers the unique lever), and applied OP to Hanabi~\cite{bard2019hanabi}. We reimplement OP in its simplest tabular, policy-gradient form and use the lever game as designed there; Hu et al.\ report that the OP-optimal policy in the lever game is the distinct lever; we do not test the optimum but whether tabular policy-gradient dynamics reach it, with a different learner and games, so our numbers are not a reproduction of theirs.

\textbf{Population-based training.} Fictitious co-play (FCP)~\cite{strouse2021collaborating} trains a pool of self-play partners and then a best-response agent against the frozen pool; maximum-entropy population training~\cite{zhao2021maximum} adds a diversity objective. Our baseline is a two-stage tabular FCP-style method without checkpoints from earlier training or human models; we call it ``Population (FCP-style)'' and it is a reimplementation, not the published system.

\textbf{Other ZSC methods.} Off-belief learning~\cite{hu2021off} grounds beliefs in a fixed base policy to avoid arbitrary conventions, K-level reasoning~\cite{cui2022k} trains a hierarchy of agents each best-responding to the previous level, and Any-Play~\cite{lucas2022any} augments training with an intrinsic diversity reward to counter the intra-algorithm limitation of cross-play. The simplified action decoder~\cite{hu2019simplified} was a Hanabi self-play baseline. None of these is run here.

\textbf{Emergent communication.} Signalling games are the standard testbed for emergent communication~\cite{lazaridou2016multi}, where arbitrary symbol conventions are the norm; our signalling game uses relabelling of messages as the symmetry. Overcooked~\cite{carroll2019utility} is a benchmark for coordination with humans that is out of reach of this CPU study.
