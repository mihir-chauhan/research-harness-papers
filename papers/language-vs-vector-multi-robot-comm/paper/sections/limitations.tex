This is a small CPU study: one task, two robots (three-robot and coverage variants are out of scope), 5 seeds, about ten seconds of training per run. Training is pathwise-gradient descent through differentiable dynamics, not reinforcement learning, so exploration and credit assignment, are absent. Baselines are simplified reimplementations, not the published systems, and no hyperparameter tuning was done per system, which may disadvantage the discrete channel: its speaker uses only 10--13 of 16 messages, and although a temperature sweep did not reliably help (Table~\ref{tab:tau}), no annealing or longer training was tried, so its ranking below the language is provisional. The templated language is a proxy: a fixed lexicon with two words, a fixed speaker and a perfectly informative template, so it says nothing about real language, ambiguity or human legibility; its cross-play robustness is built in. Cross-play compares only seeds of the same architecture. Statistical tests on 5 seeds are low-powered. A fuller study would add reinforcement-learning training, more robots and tasks, partner-adaptation or population-based training, and real or LLM-generated language.
