In a hidden-target rendezvous, every communication channel beat silence; a learned continuous vector had the highest final success, a fixed templated language came second and the (untuned) learned discrete channel third at the main capacity. Learning speed was not clearly different between the continuous vector and the language at our evaluation granularity, and both were much faster than the discrete channel. The proxy language was the only channel that survived a change of training partner, a consequence of its fixed speaker. The ranking depends on grid resolution and channel noise, so we regard the result as a controlled toy comparison rather than evidence about natural language in robot teams.
