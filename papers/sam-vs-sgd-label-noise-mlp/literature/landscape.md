# Landscape
- SAM (Foret et al. 2020) minimises the worst-case loss in an rho-ball around the weights; large gains on image classification, and notably under label noise. Baek et al. 2024 ("Why is SAM robust to label noise?") argue the gain comes from the reweighting of per-sample gradients early/late in training, not from flatness per se. Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2022 analyse SAM's implicit bias (diagonal linear networks) and show it differs from a pure flat-minimum story; Wen et al. 2022 study how SAM reduces sharpness; Kwon et al. 2021 (ASAM) fix the parameter-rescaling dependence of a fixed-radius sharpness; Du et al. 2021 (ESAM) cut the cost.
- Sharpness vs generalisation: Keskar et al. 2016 link large-batch sharp minima to a larger gap; Jiang et al. 2019 test 40+ measures; Andriushchenko et al. 2023 find the relation between sharpness and generalisation is not uniform across settings. Zhang et al. 2016 is the label-noise memorisation reference.
- Gap: most evidence is on CNNs/ResNets on GPU. We ask a controlled small-scale question: with an equal tuning protocol including weight decay, a validation-selected rho, many seeds and a fixed-norm sharpness measure, how much does SAM help, how rho-sensitive is it, and does a fixed-radius sharpness track the gap across optimisers.
