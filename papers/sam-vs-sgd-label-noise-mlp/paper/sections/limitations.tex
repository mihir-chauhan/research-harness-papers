\textbf{Scale.} One small architecture (3-layer MLP, width 128), tiny data (600 or 400 training points), two datasets, symmetric noise only, and 5 evaluation seeds: the paired and Welch tests have low power, the registered tests are uncorrected (with the Holm adjustment of Table~\ref{tab:cmp} the SAM vs SGD+WD margin at 40\% noise is no longer below 0.05), and several gaps (SAM vs SGD+WD at 20\% noise) are within one standard deviation. The results do not transfer automatically to CNNs, large data or instance-dependent noise.

\textbf{Tuning.} The learning rate, batch size, epoch count and momentum were fixed for all systems and not tuned, so a differently tuned SGD or SGD+WD could shift the margins; $\rho$ had 6 candidate values and $\lambda$ 4, selection used a noisy validation split and 3 seeds, and for noisy digits the selected $\rho=0.5$ sits next to a collapse at 1.0 so the true optimum is not resolved. On digits the tuning seeds re-split the same 1797 images as the evaluation seeds, so the images used for selection overlap the evaluation test images; this is the same for SAM and SGD+WD and only chooses one of 6 or 4 grid values, but the selection is not independent of the test data. SAM+WD was not tuned jointly. The comparison is epoch-matched, not compute-matched: SAM uses two gradient evaluations per step, and we did not run SGD or SGD+WD with twice the steps. We use the final-epoch model with no early stopping, whereas \cite{baek2024why} report that SAM's label-noise gains peak with early stopping; early-stopped SGD would be a natural, stronger baseline that we did not run.

\textbf{Sharpness measurement.} Our sharpness uses a single fixed Euclidean radius (0.5), one-step adversarial or 20 random directions, measured on the noisy training set. It is not scale invariant (cf.\ ASAM \cite{kwon2021asam}), the weight norms differ widely across systems (at 40\% noise \rhval{main/sgd+wd/digits_n0.4/weight_norm/mean:2} for SGD+WD against \rhval{main/sgd/digits_n0.4/weight_norm/mean:2} for SGD), and the training loss levels differ, so differences in $S_{\mathrm{adv}}$ across optimisers confound flatness with loss level and weight scale. The sharpness--gap correlations are observational, pool runs with different optimisers and some collapsed runs, and the exploratory ``no collapsed'' rows were defined after seeing the data. Our gap definition compares noisy-train and clean-test accuracy and is negative when a model does not memorise.

\textbf{Implementation.} All optimisers are reimplemented here from their published description, SAM uses a global-norm perturbation on every minibatch (not m-sharpness splits), and the spirals task is easy enough that no system memorises noise, which limits what it can show. A full study would add CNNs on CIFAR-N-style noise, more seeds, joint tuning, early stopping and scale-invariant sharpness.
