\textbf{H1 (SAM beats SGD and SGD+WD under noise): supported against SGD, mixed against SGD+WD.}
Without noise (digits\_n0.0) SGD, SGD+WD and SAM reach 0.959, 0.962 and 0.970; the SAM margin over SGD (0.010) has paired $p=0.00731$ but Welch $p=0.056$, i.e.\ it is small and test-dependent. At 20\% and 40\% noise plain SGD memorises essentially all flipped labels (memorised 0.997 and 1.000 in Table~\ref{tab:main}) and drops to 0.799 and 0.625; SAM reaches 0.938 and 0.901 and memorises far fewer flipped labels (0.156 at 20\%), with differences of 0.139 and 0.276 and $p<10^{-4}$ under both tests. Against tuned SGD+WD (0.931 and 0.874), however, the margins are 0.0078 at 20\% noise (paired $p=0.049$, Welch $p=0.294$; below one seed standard deviation of either system) and 0.027 at 40\% (paired $p=0.025$, Welch $p=0.0073$). The per-seed differences SAM minus SGD+WD are $0.025, 0.038, 0.036, 0.039, -0.002$ at 40\% noise (one of five seeds negative) and $0.006, 0.012, 0.011, -0.002, 0.012$ at 20\%. We therefore call the 20\% comparison inconclusive and the 40\% one supported only without multiple-comparison correction (the Holm-adjusted paired $p$ over the 8 paired tests of Table~\ref{tab:cmp} is 0.101), with the caveats that $n=5$, and SAM's $\rho$ was selected from 6 values versus 4 for $\lambda$ (both selected on a noisy validation split, so neither used the test set). Weight decay alone removes most of the damage of label noise at this scale: SGD+WD memorises 0.053 of flipped labels at 20\% noise.

\textbf{Where SAM fails.} On two-spirals (20\% noise) the model does not memorise noise at all (memorised 0.085--0.102 for every system) and the three systems are indistinguishable: SAM 0.912 vs SGD 0.919 and SGD+WD 0.914 (paired $p=0.320$ and $0.606$). With $\rho$ larger than the selected 0.05, SAM is worse there (Table~\ref{tab:rho}: 0.888 at $\rho=0.1$, 0.767 at $\rho=0.2$ and 0.518, i.e.\ chance, at $\rho=0.5$). On this task SAM gives no measurable gain, so H1 does not hold there.

\textbf{H2 (interior optimum in $\rho$, larger and wider useful range under noise): partly supported.} Table~\ref{tab:rho} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rho} show the sweep. On noise-free digits accuracy rises from 0.960 ($\rho=0.02$) to 0.974 at $\rho=0.2$ and then falls to 0.970 at 0.5 and chance-level (0.136) at 1.0, an interior optimum, but the gain over SGD (0.959 $\pm$ 0.008) is under two SGD seed standard deviations, and accuracy stays between 0.960 and 0.974 for $\rho$ from 0.02 to 0.5. On noisy digits accuracy rises monotonically up to $\rho=0.5$ (from 0.807 at $\rho=0.02$ to 0.938 at 20\% noise, from 0.629 to 0.901 at 40\%) and then collapses to chance at $\rho=1.0$ (0.103 and 0.118): the best grid value thus shifts from 0.2 without noise (0.1 and 0.5 are within 0.004 of it) to 0.5 with noise, as hypothesised, but the useful range is not wider: under noise only $\rho=0.5$ recovers most of the gain (0.2 gives 0.862 and 0.698). The optimum is only bracketed in $(0.2,1.0)$ by this grid; the selected value sits one grid step below the collapse and we tested no value other than 0.5 inside that interval, so we cannot say how close to the cliff it is. On spirals accuracy decreases monotonically from $\rho=0.02$ (0.921) to chance at $\rho\ge0.5$, so no interior optimum is observed (the registered refutation condition, a monotone curve, is met on this task). Nothing is claimed beyond these 6 grid points. The weight-decay sweep (Table~\ref{tab:wd}) has the same shape: $\lambda=0.01$ is best on noisy digits (0.931, 0.874) and $\lambda=0.05$ collapses (0.525, 0.099).

\textbf{H3 (sharpness tracks the gap): not supported.} The registered criterion required positive correlations pooled and within every task; the within-task ones are not all positive. Table~\ref{tab:corr} gives Spearman correlations between the two sharpness measures and the two gaps. Pooled over all 260 runs the correlations are weak: 0.24 for random-direction sharpness and 0.12 for adversarial sharpness with gap\_acc (0.07 for adversarial sharpness with the loss gap). Per task they differ in sign: on spirals they are negative ($-0.72$, $-0.34$), on digits\_n0.0 adversarial sharpness is negatively correlated with the loss gap ($-0.65$), and on noisy digits they are positive but modest (0.35--0.38, apart from adversarial sharpness at 20\% noise: 0.04). Only within the SAM $\rho$ sweep on digits do they reach 0.61--0.76 (90 runs), but there $\rho$ moves both quantities and the collapsed runs at large $\rho$ are included; removing collapsed runs (rows ``no collapsed'': test accuracy below 0.15 on digits or 0.55 on spirals, an exploratory step defined after seeing the data) leaves per-task values between $-0.69$ and 0.23 (digits\_n0.4: 0.10 and 0.09 for the accuracy gap). Across optimisers the adversarial measure contradicts the flatness story (the random-direction measure in Table~\ref{tab:main} ranks SAM flattest, 0.615 and 0.539 vs 1.125 and 1.353 for SGD at 20\% and 40\% noise, in units of $10^{-3}$, and SGD+WD sharpest, 1.804 and 2.424, so the two measures disagree about SGD): in Table~\ref{tab:main}, SGD has the smallest adversarial sharpness at both noisy digits tasks (0.0320 and 0.0331 vs 0.0585 and 0.0753 for SAM) yet the largest gap (0.200 vs $-0.125$ at 20\%). A fixed-radius loss increase also depends on the loss level and on the weight scale, which differ across systems (at 20\% noise SGD+WD has weight norm 8.91 vs 23.71 for SGD), so we do not interpret it as intrinsic flatness.
