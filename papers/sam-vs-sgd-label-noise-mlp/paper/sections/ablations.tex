\textbf{Random vs adversarial direction.} Replacing the ascent direction by a random direction of the same radius removes the benefit entirely: on digits at 20\% and 40\% noise the accuracy is 0.800 and 0.623, within 0.002 of SGD (0.799 and 0.625), and nearly all flipped labels are memorised (0.998 and 1.000). With the same radius and the same extra compute, the gradient-aligned direction is therefore what produces the effect here, rather than weight noise of the same Euclidean norm or the extra step. This is a weak control: a random direction in the roughly 26k-dimensional parameter space changes the loss far less than the gradient direction (for SGD at 20\% noise, a step of norm 0.5 raises the training loss by $1.125\times10^{-3}$ in a random direction against 0.0320 along the gradient, Table~\ref{tab:main}), so it does not rule out that larger random noise would help. On two-spirals and noise-free digits the random variant matches SGD too (0.919, 0.959). This isolates the perturbation direction, not the reason why the adversarial direction helps (e.g.\ clean-example up-weighting versus implicit regularisation \cite{baek2024why}), which we did not test.

\textbf{SAM+WD (failure of naive combination).} Combining the selected $\rho$ and $\lambda$ without joint tuning is harmful on noisy digits: test accuracy 0.223 $\pm$ 0.290 at 20\% noise and 0.099 at 40\% (chance; at 20\% noise four seeds are near 0.09 and one reaches 0.741, so the mean is bimodal). In the collapsed runs the weights are driven towards zero (weight norm 0.32 $\pm$ 0.07 at 40\% noise against 16.75 for SAM and 8.77 for SGD+WD). Without noise SAM+WD is comparable to SAM (0.972 $\pm$ 0.006 vs 0.970 $\pm$ 0.006), and on spirals it is 0.901 against 0.912 for SAM, within one seed standard deviation. On noisy digits each regulariser was selected alone at the grid value just below its own collapse (Tables~\ref{tab:rho},\ref{tab:wd}), so together they may over-regularise; we did not run a joint grid, so we cannot say whether a jointly tuned SAM+WD would beat SAM or SGD+WD.

\textbf{Sensitivity.} Both regularisers show a cliff just above their best value on noisy digits ($\rho$: 0.938 to 0.103 from 0.5 to 1.0 at 20\% noise; $\lambda$: 0.931 to 0.525 from 0.01 to 0.05). SAM's best value gains only 0.0078 over SGD+WD's at 20\% noise. The practical reading is that at this scale a badly chosen $\rho$ (or $\lambda$) can be much worse than plain SGD, and that the comparison with a tuned weight decay is the relevant one.
