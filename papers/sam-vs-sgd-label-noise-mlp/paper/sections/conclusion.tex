On a small MLP with symmetric label noise, SAM improved clean test accuracy over plain SGD by a large margin (0.938 vs 0.799 and 0.901 vs 0.625 at 20\% and 40\% noise on digits), but weight decay alone recovered most of that gain, leaving a margin of 0.0078 at 20\% noise (inconclusive) and 0.027 at 40\% (both tests below 0.05 uncorrected with $n=5$, one seed negative). SAM gave no benefit on noisy spirals, was sensitive to $\rho$, with a collapse to chance one grid step above the best value on noisy digits, and a random-direction perturbation of the same radius behaved like SGD. A fixed-radius loss-increase sharpness did not track the generalisation gap across optimisers (pooled Spearman 0.07--0.24, negative within spirals). Within the limits of a five-seed, epoch-matched CPU study with untuned learning rate, the practical take-away is to compare SAM against a tuned weight decay and to tune $\rho$ on validation data; we make no claim about larger models.
