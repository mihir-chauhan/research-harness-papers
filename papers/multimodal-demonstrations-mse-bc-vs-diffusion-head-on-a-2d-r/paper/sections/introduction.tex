Behavior cloning (BC) fits a policy to demonstrated state--action pairs. When several actions are valid in the same state, a policy trained with a mean-squared-error (MSE) loss predicts their average, which can be an invalid action; this is the textbook failure that motivates multimodal policy classes~\cite{florence2021implicit,shafiullah2022behavior,urain2024deep}. Diffusion Policy~\cite{chi2023diffusion}, which represents the policy as a conditional denoising diffusion model~\cite{ho2020denoising} over action sequences, is now a common choice and is reported to model multimodal action distributions better than the alternatives it was compared with, including implicit and transformer-based discrete-plus-offset heads~\cite{florence2021implicit,shafiullah2022behavior}.

However, in a real benchmark many things change at once between heads (network, chunking, observation encoder, training budget), and a plain Gaussian-mixture head, the simplest multimodal baseline already used in play-based imitation~\cite{lynch2019learning}, is often absent or weakly tuned. We therefore isolate the question in the smallest setting that has genuine multimodality: a point mass that must go around a circular obstacle on its left or its right, with demonstrations split between the two sides. All heads share the same data, MLP width, optimiser and epoch budget.

We ask five questions (H1--H5, Section~\ref{sec:setup}): does MSE fail, does DDPM beat a two-component mixture, do the heads reproduce the demonstrated mode proportions, how does the answer depend on start-state variation, and how much do the number of denoising steps and the action-chunk length matter. Our contributions are (i) a controlled, fully reproducible comparison across 3 tasks and 10 seeds with success, collision and per-mode metrics; (ii) a sensitivity sweep showing that the regression failure is tied to how much the start states differ; (iii) negative findings: the DDPM head gives no measurable gain over a two-component mixture here, and it is more fragile to open-loop action chunking. All code, runs and numbers are in the accompanying run registry. The study is small by design (CPU, MLPs, one environment) and its limits are listed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
