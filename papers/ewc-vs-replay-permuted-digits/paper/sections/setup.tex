\textbf{Data.} The 1797 scikit-learn digit images (8$\times$8, scaled to $[0,1]$) are split per seed into stratified 60/20/20 train/validation/test sets; each task uses the subset of the split that belongs to it (about 1078 training images per permuted task, about 215 per split task). The permutations, class pairing and initialisation also change with the seed.

\textbf{Model and optimisation (all systems).} MLP 64--100--100--10 with ReLU, plain SGD, batch size 32, 30 epochs per task, gradient-norm clipping at 5. Clipping was added for all systems after large-$\lambda$ EWC diverged to non-finite values with unclipped SGD in a first smoke test. The learning rate was chosen from $\{0.01,0.05,0.1\}$ by validation average accuracy of fine-tuning and joint training on two separate seeds (100, 101; Table~\ref{tab:lr}): 0.1 was best for joint training on every scenario and for fine-tuning on perm\_dil, the three rates were within 0.001 for fine-tuning on split\_cil, and 0.01 was better for fine-tuning on split\_til (0.930 against 0.911). 0.1 is used by every system. It was not re-tuned for EWC or ER.

\textbf{Seeds and selection.} The main comparison uses seeds 0--4. The sweeps use seeds 10--14 (different splits and permutations), $M\in\{0,5,10,20,50,100,200,500\}$ and $\lambda\in\{0,1,10,100,10^3,10^4,10^5\}$. The EWC strength reported in the main table is the sweep value with the highest mean \emph{validation} average accuracy per scenario ($\lambda{=}10$ for perm\_dil, $10^3$ for split\_cil and split\_til); the ER sizes $M{=}20$ and $M{=}100$ were fixed in advance. The test split was never used for selection. Because the main and sweep seeds differ, sweep rows are not the same runs as main rows.

\textbf{Compute.} CPU only, 2 threads, a shared macOS machine under heavy load. Individual runs take seconds to tens of seconds; the learning-rate selection, sweeps (225 runs) and main experiment (75 runs) took roughly 5, 18 and 5 minutes of wall-clock. Runs are logged with \texttt{rh run} and the code commit is recorded for each; the job list of every group is \texttt{experiments/jobs.py}. The sweeps were executed twice (see Limitations); the timing above is that of the first execution. Statistics: mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over seeds; paired $t$-tests over the five seeds from \texttt{rh compare}, and 95\% $t$-intervals of the same paired differences from \texttt{experiments/paired\_stats.py}, with no multiple-comparison correction, so $p$-values are indicative only.
