We compare naive fine-tuning, elastic weight consolidation (EWC), experience replay (ER) and joint training on five-task sequences built from the 8$\times$8 scikit-learn digits: permuted pixels (domain-incremental), and split classes in class-incremental and task-incremental form. All systems share one MLP, optimiser, epoch budget (except joint training) and evaluation, and we report final average accuracy and forgetting over five seeds, plus sweeps of the replay buffer size $M$ and of the EWC strength $\lambda$ on separate seeds. A 100-sample buffer (25 stored examples per earlier task while the last task is learned) reached higher average accuracy than the validation-selected EWC on permuted digits (\rhval{main/experience-replay-m-100/perm_dil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/ewc/perm_dil/average_accuracy/mean:3}) and class-incremental split digits (\rhval{main/experience-replay-m-100/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/ewc/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3}), while EWC was only slightly above fine-tuning in the class-incremental case (\rhval{main/ewc/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/fine-tuning/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3}, within noise), and on the separate sweep seeds the same configuration reached \rhval{sweep_lambda/ewc@lam=1000/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} against \rhval{sweep_lambda/ewc@lam=0/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3}. With task identity at test time all methods are close (\rhval{main/fine-tuning/split_til/average_accuracy/mean:3}--\rhval{main/joint-training/split_til/average_accuracy/mean:3}), and five seeds do not separate replay from EWC or from joint training. Accuracy rises with $M$ in the two harder scenarios, whereas EWC accuracy peaks at an interior $\lambda$ and its low forgetting at large $\lambda$ comes with low accuracy on the last task. This is a small CPU study with reimplemented baselines, one architecture and five seeds; it replicates known qualitative findings rather than extending them.
