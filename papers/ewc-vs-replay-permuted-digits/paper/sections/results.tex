\textbf{H1 (permuted digits, ER vs EWC): supported.} ER with $M{=}100$ reaches 0.847 average accuracy against 0.695 for EWC (difference 0.152, $p{=}1.2\times10^{-4}$) and has lower forgetting (0.148 vs 0.303). EWC also has the lowest last-task accuracy of the five systems, 0.917 against 0.976 for ER and 0.973 for fine-tuning. The claim does not extend to small buffers: ER with $M{=}20$ (0.670) is below EWC (0.695), a difference of $-0.025$ that five seeds do not resolve ($p{=}0.092$). Joint training reaches 0.954, 0.107 above ER with $M{=}100$, so a 100-sample buffer does not close the gap to joint training.

\textbf{H2 (class-incremental split digits): partly supported, seed-dependent.} Fine-tuning gets 0.214 with forgetting 0.973 and last-task accuracy 0.997, the pattern expected of a network that predicts only the two most recent classes. EWC with $\lambda{=}10^3$ reaches 0.246, a gain of 0.032, below the registered 0.05 threshold but within noise ($p{=}0.220$; the 95\% interval $[-0.029, 0.094]$ contains the threshold). The EWC half of H2 therefore holds only by the registered mean criterion on the main seeds: with the identical configuration ($\lambda{=}10^3$) on the sweep seeds 10--14, EWC reaches 0.340 against 0.196 for fine-tuning (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}), a gain well above 0.05. We do not reconcile the two seed sets beyond noting that the EWC result on this scenario is highly seed-dependent. The ER half holds: ER with $M{=}100$ reaches 0.896 (gain 0.682 over fine-tuning and 0.650 over EWC, both $p{<}10^{-4}$), and ER with $M{=}20$ reaches 0.728. This is consistent with class-incremental learning being the hardest scenario in \cite{ven2019three}.

\textbf{Failure case: EWC on split\_cil.} EWC does not merely fail to gain here. Its last-task accuracy is 0.118 $\pm$ 0.263: in the run registry it is exactly zero in four of the five seeds, that is, no test image of the fifth class pair is classified correctly after training on it, and non-zero in the remaining seed. At this $\lambda$ the network therefore keeps part of the earlier tasks (forgetting 0.568 against 0.973 for fine-tuning) and does not acquire the last one, which leaves the average near the fine-tuning level. We did not run an experiment that isolates the cause; that the summed penalties of four earlier tasks are too stiff for the single shared output layer may explain it, but this remains a hypothesis. The sweep (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}) shows no $\lambda$ above 0.340 average accuracy on this scenario.

\textbf{H3 (task-incremental split digits): supported, but the scenario is nearly saturated.} Fine-tuning reaches 0.946 and joint training 0.990, a gap of 0.044, inside the registered 0.10. ER ($M{=}100$) 0.984, ER ($M{=}20$) 0.973 and EWC 0.973 lie between them. ER with $M{=}100$ is not separated from fine-tuning at the 0.05 level ($p{=}0.050$, interval from $-0.000$ to 0.075), from EWC ($p{=}0.155$) or from joint training ($p{=}0.258$). Joint training is above fine-tuning ($p{=}0.023$) and above EWC ($p{=}0.033$); these two uncorrected values would not survive a correction for the twelve tests of Table~\ref{tab:paired}. We therefore cannot rank the three continual methods here. EWC's last-task accuracy (0.945 $\pm$ 0.071) is below the 0.997 that the other four systems share.

\textbf{H5 (joint upper bound): supported in the means, not significantly on split\_til.} Joint training has the highest average accuracy in every scenario (0.954, 0.957, 0.990). Against ER with $M{=}100$ it is ahead by 0.107 on perm\_dil and by 0.061 on split\_cil (both $p{<}0.01$), and by 0.006 on split\_til with $p{=}0.258$. The joint-training baseline retrains with more optimisation steps than the sequential methods, so it is an upper bound in data, not in compute.

\textbf{Forgetting versus accuracy.} Low forgetting did not imply high accuracy. On split\_til EWC has the second-lowest forgetting (0.006) together with the lowest last-task accuracy, and on split\_cil it has lower forgetting than fine-tuning with almost the same average accuracy. The sweep in Section~\ref{sec:ablations} shows the extreme case, forgetting near zero at the largest $\lambda$ with low average accuracy. We therefore read forgetting together with average and last-task accuracy.
