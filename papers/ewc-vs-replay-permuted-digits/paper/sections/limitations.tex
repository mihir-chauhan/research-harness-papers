\textbf{Scale.} The data are 8$\times$8 digits (1797 images), the network is a 17k-parameter MLP, tasks have about 200--1000 training images and there are only five tasks. Five seeds give wide uncertainty on small differences, as in split\_til; the $p$-values are uncorrected and use a normality assumption on five points.

\textbf{Baselines and tuning.} EWC and ER are our reimplementations, not the original code; EWC uses a sampled-label diagonal Fisher with one penalty per task, and other variants (online EWC, exact Fisher, SI) might behave differently. All systems share one learning rate chosen on fine-tuning and joint training only; EWC and replay might prefer other optimiser settings, epochs, or no gradient clipping, and the $\lambda$ and $M$ grids are coarse (factors of 10 and about 2). Clipping was introduced to avoid divergence and may itself limit EWC's effective stiffness at large $\lambda$.

\textbf{Design choices that affect the comparison.} Replay sees a batch of 32 buffer samples per step regardless of $M$, so a small buffer is repeated many times in 30 epochs; ER and joint training also see more gradient steps or data than fine-tuning and EWC. In the buffer, per-task shares shrink as tasks arrive. We compare the knobs $M$ and $\lambda$ but not the memory cost in bytes or compute time. The class-incremental EWC failure is shown only for one validation-selected $\lambda$ plus the sweep; we did not isolate its mechanism. Task identity masking is one way of building a task-incremental setting; other choices (multi-head with separate output layers) were not tried. Results on digits need not transfer to MNIST, CIFAR or pretrained models; a full study would add larger benchmarks, more seeds, longer task sequences, other baselines (SI, LwF, A-GEM, DER++) and tuned hyperparameters for every method.


\textbf{Data reuse.} Every seed re-splits the same 1797 images, so the test images of the main seeds overlap the training and validation images of the seeds used to select the learning rate and $\lambda$ (and of the sweep seeds); the selection is therefore not independent of the test images, although no test metric was used for it.

\textbf{Provenance.} In the first execution of the sweeps two workers started jobs in the same second and some rows shared a log file. Those rows are retired in the run registry and all sweep runs (\rhval{count/sweep_buffer/runs} buffer and \rhval{count/sweep_lambda/runs} $\lambda$ runs) were executed again with one log per row; the re-run metrics are identical to the retired ones, and every sweep number in this paper is from the re-run. The learning-rate and main runs were not repeated.
