On five-task sequences from the scikit-learn digits, a replay buffer of 100 stored samples beat validation-tuned EWC in the two scenarios without task identity (\rhval{main/experience-replay-m-100/perm_dil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/ewc/perm_dil/average_accuracy/mean:3} on permuted digits and \rhval{main/experience-replay-m-100/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/ewc/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} on class-incremental split digits), and EWC was only slightly above fine-tuning in the class-incremental case (\rhval{main/ewc/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/fine-tuning/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3}, within noise; \rhval{sweep_lambda/ewc@lam=1000/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} against \rhval{sweep_lambda/ewc@lam=0/split_cil/average_accuracy/mean:3} on the separate sweep seeds). With task identity at test time all methods were close to joint training (\rhval{main/fine-tuning/split_til/average_accuracy/mean:3}--\rhval{main/joint-training/split_til/average_accuracy/mean:3}) and the differences among the three continual methods were not resolvable with five seeds. Replay accuracy increased with the buffer size in the two harder scenarios, while EWC showed an interior optimum in $\lambda$ and traded forgetting for plasticity at large $\lambda$. These results are in the spirit of earlier comparisons \cite{ven2019three,chaudhry2019tiny}, at small scale with our own reimplementations; they should not be read as a statement about larger problems or about EWC variants we did not run.
