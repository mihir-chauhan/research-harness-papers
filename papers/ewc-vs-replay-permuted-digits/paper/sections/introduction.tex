Neural networks trained on a sequence of tasks tend to lose performance on earlier tasks, a problem known as catastrophic forgetting \cite{goodfellow2013empirical,kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Two families of remedies are widely used as baselines. Regularisation methods such as elastic weight consolidation (EWC) \cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} add a quadratic penalty that anchors parameters judged important for earlier tasks, and related penalties include synaptic intelligence \cite{zenke2017continual} and a reparametrised variant of EWC \cite{liu2018rotate}. Memory-based methods keep a few stored examples and use them while learning new tasks \cite{lopezpaz2017gradient,chaudhry2018efficient,rebuffi2017icarl,buzzega2020dark}; Chaudhry et al.\ report that a simple baseline that trains jointly on the current data and a very small episodic memory outperforms several specifically designed methods when each example is seen once \cite{chaudhry2019tiny}.

How hard the problem is depends on the scenario. van de Ven and Tolias distinguish task-, domain- and class-incremental learning according to whether task identity is given at test time and, if not, whether it must be inferred, and find substantial differences in difficulty and in method efficiency between them, class-incremental learning being the case where identity must be inferred \cite{ven2019three}. Their benchmarks use permuted and split MNIST. We ask the same question at a much smaller scale that fits a few CPU minutes: on sequences built from the scikit-learn digits, how do fine-tuning, EWC, replay and joint training compare, and how does the single knob of each method, the buffer size $M$ for replay and the strength $\lambda$ for EWC, trade off?

\textbf{Hypotheses.} Registered in \texttt{proposal.md} before the runs: H1, ER with $M{=}100$ beats EWC on permuted digits; H2, on class-incremental split digits EWC gains at most 0.05 over fine-tuning while ER gains more than 0.2; H3, with task identity fine-tuning is within 0.10 of joint training; H4, replay accuracy increases with $M$ and EWC accuracy is an inverted-U in $\lambda$; H5, joint training upper-bounds every continual system.

\textbf{Contribution.} A controlled comparison with matched settings and two sensitivity sweeps; the job lists of every run group are in the repository (\texttt{experiments/jobs.py}). We propose no new algorithm, and both EWC and ER are reimplemented here rather than taken from the original code.
