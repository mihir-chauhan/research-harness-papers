\textbf{H4a, replay accuracy grows with $M$: supported.} In perm\_dil and split\_cil the mean accuracy increases at every step of the grid, from 0.493 ($M{=}0$) to 0.921 ($M{=}500$) and from 0.196 to 0.933. In split\_til $M{=}500$ (0.989) exceeds $M{=}20$ (0.971) and $M{=}0$ (0.909), but the sequence is not strictly monotone ($M{=}20$ 0.971, $M{=}50$ 0.968), and these differences are of the order of the seed standard deviation. In split\_cil even $M{=}5$ (one example per task in the last stage) lifts accuracy from 0.196 to 0.416.

\textbf{H4b, EWC is inverted-U in $\lambda$: supported on perm\_dil and split\_cil, with a weak peak on the latter.} On perm\_dil accuracy peaks at $\lambda{=}10$ (0.669) and falls on both sides to 0.493 ($\lambda{=}0$) and 0.273 ($\lambda{=}10^5$), while forgetting falls monotonically to 0.007. The low forgetting is paid for on the new task (Table~\ref{tab:last}): last-task accuracy on perm\_dil drops from 0.961 at $\lambda\le1$ to 0.765 at $\lambda{=}100$ and to 0.126 or less for $\lambda\ge10^3$, whereas it stays between 0.958 and 0.962 for the replay sizes shown. On split\_cil the maximum is 0.340 at $\lambda{=}10^3$, between flat values near 0.2 at $\lambda\le100$ and at $10^5$; the peak exceeds its neighbours by many seed standard deviations (0.008) but lies far below every replay buffer with $M\ge20$ (0.730 or more). For $\lambda\ge10^4$ the last-task accuracy on split\_cil is exactly zero in all five seeds (0.000 $\pm$ 0.000 in Table~\ref{tab:last}): no test image of the last class pair is classified correctly, the same failure as in the main runs. On split\_til the optimum is also interior ($\lambda{=}10^3$, 0.973), and $\lambda\ge10^4$ is worse than fine-tuning (0.875 and 0.715 against 0.909), with last-task accuracy down to 0.807 and 0.518.

\textbf{Memory versus strength.} Compared with the best EWC value of each scenario, replay passes it at $M{=}20$ on perm\_dil (0.684 vs 0.669, a difference smaller than the seed std; $M{=}10$ gives 0.597), already at $M{=}5$ on split\_cil (0.416 vs 0.340), and only reaches it at $M{=}20$ on split\_til (0.971 vs 0.973, a tie). In each scenario the best EWC value in Table~\ref{tab:sweep} is also the validation-selected one used in the main table, and it is the best of seven values on these seeds, whereas the buffer sizes were not tuned. The relative cost is not measured: ER stores raw examples and needs a second forward pass per step, EWC stores one Fisher and one parameter copy per task, and we did not time either.
