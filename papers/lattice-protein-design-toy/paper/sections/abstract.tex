We study inverse design on the exactly solvable two-dimensional HP lattice model with chains of 16 residues: given a target conformation, return a hydrophobic/polar sequence whose \emph{unique} minimum-energy conformation is the target. We enumerate all \rhval{data/enumeration/hp16/n_conformations/mean} self-avoiding conformations (modulo lattice symmetry), score every sequence exactly, and compare a conditional encoder--decoder autoregressive designer, trained on pairs of conformations and uniquely folding sequences, with reimplemented simulated annealing, random search and a one-line contact heuristic. All systems get the same budget $K$ of ground-state-oracle calls per design and the same held-out targets, split by chain-reversal class to avoid a symmetry leak; we use five seeds. With $K=100$ the autoregressive designer succeeds on \rhval{main/conditional-ar-designer/hp16/succ_k100/mean:3} of held-out targets, against \rhval{main/simulated-annealing/hp16/succ_k100/mean:3} for simulated annealing with the registered binary degeneracy objective, but the picture is mixed: the contact heuristic alone reaches \rhval{main/contact-heuristic/hp16/succ_k1/mean:3} with a single design and with no oracle call, which the autoregressive model at $K=1$ (\rhval{main/conditional-ar-designer/hp16/succ_k1/mean:3}) does not beat, and exploratory simulated-annealing variants added after seeing the main results change the picture: with a graded degeneracy penalty, cold-start annealing reaches \rhval{extra_sa_log/sa-graded-objective/hp16/succ_k1000/mean:3} at $K=1000$ (not distinguishable from the autoregressive model at \rhval{main/conditional-ar-designer/hp16/succ_k1000/mean:3} in our five seeds), and graded annealing started from the contact-heuristic sequence reaches \rhval{extra_sa_log/sa-graded-objective-heuristic-init/hp16/succ_k1000/mean:3}, better than the autoregressive model (paired $p=$ \rhval{analysis_paired/paired-comparison/hp16/ar_vs_salogw_k1000_p/mean:3}). Against this strongest search baseline the autoregressive designer's advantage is established only at $K=10$ ($p=$ \rhval{analysis_paired/paired-comparison/hp16/ar_vs_salogw_k10_p/mean:3}); at $K=100$ the difference is not detectable ($p=$ \rhval{analysis_paired/paired-comparison/hp16/ar_vs_salogw_k100_p/mean:3}). Conditioning on the target is essential, and the headline comparison depends strongly on the objective and initialisation given to the baseline. Reversal augmentation is a no-op at full data (the reversed pairs are already in the training set) and helps only in a post-hoc follow-up at small data fractions. This is a toy-scale CPU study; nothing here transfers to real proteins without further evidence.
