In a controlled tabular testbed the count bonuses and the penalty-only control found a sparse reward far faster than $\epsilon$-greedy as chains and room grids grew. The control shows that the constant step penalty they share (optimistic initialisation), not the count signal, explains the gain: counts without the penalty fail on the larger tasks, and counts with it are within noise of the penalty alone on the noise-free tasks. Our RND-style bonus, once its normaliser was guarded, found the reward in 39 of 40 runs but was slower than the penalty alone on every task. The unguarded normaliser is a concrete failure case for small reimplementations: a division by a zero running standard deviation produced an early bonus spike in 20 of 40 runs, and all 19 runs that never found the reward were among them. The noisy TV drew slightly more visits as its token set grew but delayed neither RND nor the observation count, as expected for these two bonuses. We read this as evidence about small deterministic gridworlds and one untuned RND-style implementation; the main follow-ups are stochastic dynamics, a forward-model bonus for the noisy-TV test, and more seeds.
