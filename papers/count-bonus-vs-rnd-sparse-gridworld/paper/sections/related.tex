\textbf{Count-based exploration.} MBIE-EB adds a bonus proportional to $1/\sqrt{n(s,a)}$ to the reward of a model-based learner \cite{strehl2008analysis}. Pseudo-counts from a density model extend such bonuses beyond tabular settings \cite{bellemare2016unifying,ostrovski2017count}; other work counts hashed states \cite{tang2016exploration} or counts in the feature space used for value approximation \cite{martin2017count}. Our count bonus is the tabular $1/\sqrt{N}$ case on arrival states.

\textbf{Optimistic initialisation.} Rashid et al.\ point out that provably efficient tabular model-free algorithms rely on optimistic initialisation, that deep Q-networks start pessimistic, and propose count-based optimism added to the Q-values \cite{rashid2020optimistic}. Our penalty-only control is the plain tabular version of this idea: with $Q_0=0$ and a negative step reward every untried action is optimistic.

\textbf{Prediction-error bonuses and the noisy TV.} Pathak et al.\ reward the error of a forward model in a learned feature space \cite{pathak2017curiosity}; Burda et al.\ study such curiosity at scale and show that it is attracted to stochastic observations \cite{burda2018large}. RND replaces the forward model by the distillation of a fixed random network, whose target is deterministic, so that stochastic transitions are not a source of irreducible error \cite{burda2018exploration}. Later work makes forward-model curiosity itself robust, by estimating aleatoric uncertainty \cite{mavorparker2021how} or by conditioning predictions on hindsight representations of the noise \cite{jarrett2022curiosity}. We have no forward-model bonus, so our noisy-TV test asks whether the RND-style and observation-count bonuses stay robust, not whether curiosity fails.

\textbf{Comparisons and deep exploration.} Ta\"iga et al.\ compare bonus methods with one agent and one protocol on Atari and find higher scores on Montezuma's Revenge but no meaningful gain over $\epsilon$-greedy otherwise \cite{taga2021bonus}. Bootstrapped DQN shows that $\epsilon$-greedy dithering can need exponentially more data than temporally extended exploration on chain-like problems \cite{osband2016deep}, which motivates our chain tasks.

\textbf{Gap.} We are not aware of a study that holds a tabular learner fixed and swaps only the bonus, with offset controls, on growing chains and rooms with a parametrised noisy-TV cell; our search was not exhaustive.
