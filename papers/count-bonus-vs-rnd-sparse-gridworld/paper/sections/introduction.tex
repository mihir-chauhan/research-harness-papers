Exploration in sparse-reward problems is a long-standing bottleneck: with a single reward at the end of a long corridor, dithering strategies such as $\epsilon$-greedy need a number of steps that grows rapidly with the distance to the goal \cite{osband2016deep}. Intrinsic bonuses address this by rewarding novelty. Count-based bonuses reward rarely visited states \cite{strehl2008analysis,bellemare2016unifying,tang2016exploration,ostrovski2017count}, while prediction-error bonuses such as curiosity \cite{pathak2017curiosity,burda2018large} and random network distillation (RND) \cite{burda2018exploration} reward states a learned model cannot yet predict. Forward-prediction bonuses are known to be attracted to inherently unpredictable observations, the ``noisy TV'' \cite{burda2018large,mavorparker2021how}; RND was designed to avoid this, because its target is a deterministic function of the observation \cite{burda2018exploration}, and an exact count on the true state is immune by construction.

The best-known comparisons of these bonuses are on Atari. A reassessment in a common framework found that bonus methods scored higher on Montezuma's Revenge but gave no meaningful gain over $\epsilon$-greedy otherwise \cite{taga2021bonus}. Here we ask a smaller question in a tabular setting where the learner is held fixed: how do $\epsilon$-greedy, count bonuses and an RND-style bonus compare in steps to first reward and final return as chains and multi-room grids grow, and does a noisy-TV cell distract them?

Contributions: (i) a compact numpy testbed with reimplementations of the three schemes and logged bonus diagnostics; (ii) a comparison on eight tasks with five seeds and two controls that separate the bonus from the optimistic offset it is combined with; (iii) an ablation of the RND normaliser on all eight tasks, which documents and isolates a numerical failure of our first implementation, and sweeps over the bonus clip, the bonus scale and the TV token cardinality. Four hypotheses are stated in Section~\ref{sec:setup}. The headline is negative for the bonuses: in these deterministic gridworlds a constant step penalty with zero-initialised Q-values explains the gain over $\epsilon$-greedy, the count signal adds nothing measurable on the noise-free tasks, and the RND-style signal makes exploration slower. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} explains why this should not be generalised.
