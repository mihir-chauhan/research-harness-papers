\emph{Failure case: the unguarded normaliser.} The first version of this study used $b=e/(\sigma_e+10^{-8})$ from the first step. When the first arrival observations coincide (the agent stays on the start cell or bumps into a wall) the errors are identical, because the predictor is first updated after eight transitions, so $\sigma_e=0$ and the bonus is $e/10^{-8}$. Table~\ref{tab:diag} shows the consequence: 20 of the 40 unguarded runs have such a spike, with bonuses up to \rhval{main/rnd-unguarded-normaliser-v1/chain_20/bonus_max/max} and Q-values up to \rhval{main/rnd-unguarded-normaliser-v1/chain_10/q_abs_max/max}, and only 1 of these 20 finds the reward (chain\_10, after \rhval{main/rnd-unguarded-normaliser-v1/chain_10/steps_to_first_reward/max} of 30000 steps), whereas all 20 runs without a spike find it. Fig.~\ref{fig:spike} shows the two groups, separated by four orders of magnitude in the early bonus (the spike threshold of $10^4$ was set after seeing this gap). The intervention confirms the cause: with only the clip, or only the warm-up, no run has a spike and 39 and 38 of 40 runs find the reward. The failures of the unguarded variant in Table~\ref{tab:main} (for example only 2 of 5 seeds succeed on room\_4 and on room\_6) are therefore a numerical artefact of the normaliser and say nothing about the novelty signal.

\emph{Guarded variants.} Guarded, clip-only and warm-up-only RND behave alike (Table~\ref{tab:main}): each finds the reward in 38 or 39 of 40 runs, and their means differ less from each other than from the unguarded variant. Clip-only is the slowest of the three on the noise-free chains and on room\_4\_tv (for example \rhval{main/rnd-clip-only/chain_10/steps_to_first_reward/mean}, \rhval{main/rnd-clip-only/chain_40/steps_to_first_reward/mean} and \rhval{main/rnd-clip-only/room_4_tv/steps_to_first_reward/mean} steps on chain\_10, chain\_40 and room\_4\_tv against \rhval{main/rnd-bonus/chain_10/steps_to_first_reward/mean}, \rhval{main/rnd-bonus/chain_40/steps_to_first_reward/mean} and \rhval{main/rnd-bonus/room_4_tv/steps_to_first_reward/mean} for the guarded method). The warm-up alone still lets the bonus reach \rhval{main/rnd-warm-up-only/room_4/bonus_max/max} (on room\_4), without visible harm. All three are slower than the penalty-only control on every task.

\emph{No normalisation.} The raw error never exceeds \rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/chain_20_tv/bonus_max/max} and is never above the offset of 1 (the share of steps with $b>1$ is zero in every run, Table~\ref{tab:diag}), so this variant is a step penalty of about $\beta$ with a small perturbation. Its means track the penalty-only control (for example \rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/chain_40/steps_to_first_reward/mean} against \rhval{main/step-penalty-only-optimistic-init/chain_40/steps_to_first_reward/mean} on chain\_40 and \rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/room_6/steps_to_first_reward/mean} against \rhval{main/step-penalty-only-optimistic-init/room_6/steps_to_first_reward/mean} on room\_6), with deviations in both directions: faster on chain\_10 (\rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/chain_10/steps_to_first_reward/mean} against \rhval{main/step-penalty-only-optimistic-init/chain_10/steps_to_first_reward/mean}) and room\_4\_tv (\rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/room_4_tv/steps_to_first_reward/mean} against \rhval{main/step-penalty-only-optimistic-init/room_4_tv/steps_to_first_reward/mean}), slower on room\_4 (\rhval{main/no-bonus-normalisation-rnd_nonorm/room_4/steps_to_first_reward/mean} against \rhval{main/step-penalty-only-optimistic-init/room_4/steps_to_first_reward/mean}). It finds the reward in all 40 runs, but this shows that the raw error is too small to matter, not that the novelty signal works.

\emph{Why is normalised RND slower than no bonus?} The normalised bonus is close to zero most of the time and exceeds the offset on a small share of the steps (rows ``median $b$'' and ``share $b>1$'' of Table~\ref{tab:diag}, which give the task where each is largest). Such positive shaped rewards make recently novel cells attractive to revisit, which may be what costs time; we did not isolate this.

The differences between clip values are of the order of one standard deviation and were not tested; the main result does not hinge on $c=5$.
