\textbf{H3 (less sensitive to weight): refuted.} The registered test is the spread of the success rate across weights, and it is the same for the two costs on each task (pendulum 0.17 vs 0.17; cart-pole 0.02 vs 0.02). Effort tells a mixed story: the energy cost's effort varies more than the quadratic cost's on the pendulum (range 5.3 vs 2.0) and less on the cart-pole (15.2 vs 52.4). The two weights are not commensurable ($w_E$ multiplies a squared energy, $q$ a squared state error), so a ``same decade'' comparison is only indicative.

\textbf{Success depends on the weight.} Both costs improve with larger weights on the pendulum: $w_E\ge 10$ and $q=100$ give success 1.00 in the 3 seeds, above the default-weight values. With the best swept weight iLQR-Quad is also at 1.00, which removes the H1 gap.

\textbf{Effort across the sweep.} Table~\ref{tab:paired} compares iLQR-Quad at every $q$ with iLQR-Energy at its default $w_E=1$, paired over seeds 0--2. On the pendulum the quadratic cost uses more effort at every $q$ (by 2.8 to 4.7). On the cart-pole the difference is 31.5 at the default $q=1$ and 55.4 to 59.1 for larger $q$, but only 6.7 and 6.9 for $q=0.01$ and $q=0.1$, with paired $p$ of 0.046 and 0.090 at $n=3$. The reverse reading matters too: iLQR-Energy at $w_E=0.01$, 0.1 and 100 (51.6, 50.9, 58.5) is not below iLQR-Quad at $q\le 0.1$ (50.0, 50.2). So on the cart-pole the energy cost is nominally cheaper than the re-weighted quadratic cost only at $w_E=1$ and $w_E=10$ (43.3 and 48.3), by a margin that 3 seeds do not clearly resolve.
