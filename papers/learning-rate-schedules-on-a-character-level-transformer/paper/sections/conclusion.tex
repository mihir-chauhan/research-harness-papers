In a two-layer character-level GPT trained for \rhval{const/steps} steps, warmup+cosine beats constant LR at its best peak rate, but the advantage vanishes at a low rate ($10^{-3}$) and reverses at $3\times10^{-4}$, and a large part of the apparent advantage at high peak rates is explained by warmup, which the constant and step-decay baselines lacked as specified. Schedule-Free AdamW (our reimplementation) reached the lowest loss (\rhval{main/schedule-free-adamw/lr1e-2/val_loss/mean:2}) but was \emph{more} sensitive to the peak LR over our grid than warmup+cosine, so the hypothesis that it is easier to tune is refuted here. Constant LR with a modest warmup had the smallest three-rate sensitivity of all configurations, at a cost in loss. These findings are for one tiny model, three seeds, and untuned secondary hyperparameters; a larger study would add more seeds, tune warmup per schedule, extend the peak-LR grid upward, test other budgets, and compare the reimplementation with the reference Schedule-Free code.
