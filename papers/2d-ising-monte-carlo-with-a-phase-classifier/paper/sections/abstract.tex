We ask how accurately classifiers trained only deep inside the ordered and disordered phases of the 2D Ising model recover the critical temperature, and whether a finite-size-scaling (FSS) collapse of their output helps. On Swendsen--Wang samples for $L=16,24,32$ at \rhval{const/n_temps} temperatures ($\rhval{const/n_samples_per_t}$ samples each, \rhval{const/n_chains} independent chains) we compare reimplemented logistic regression (raw and Z2-gauge-fixed), an MLP, a small periodic CNN, PCA and learning by confusion, plus a non-ML susceptibility/Binder reference, over \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds against the Onsager value $T_c=\rhval{const/tc_exact}$.
At $L=32$ the 0.5 crossing of the CNN and the MLP is within \rhval{main/cnn/ising/tc_error_l32/mean:3} and \rhval{main/mlp/ising/tc_error_l32/mean:3} of $T_c$ on average, better than PCA (\rhval{main/pca/ising/tc_error_l32/mean:3}; paired Wilcoxon $p=\rhval{analysis/paired-tests/ising/h1_cnn_vs_pca_l32_p/mean:3}$ for the CNN) and not distinguishable from learning by confusion, which has the nominally lowest error at $L=32$ (\rhval{main/confusion-mlp/ising/tc_error_l32/mean:3}; paired Wilcoxon $p=\rhval{analysis/paired-tests/ising/conf_vs_cnn_l32_p/mean:2}$ against the CNN and $\rhval{analysis/paired-tests/ising/conf_vs_mlp_l32_p/mean:2}$ against the MLP); the temperature grid spacing is \rhval{const/temp_step:3}.
Logistic regression on raw spins is at chance (held-out accuracy \rhval{main/logreg-raw/ising/acc_far/mean:2}) because of the spin-flip symmetry, and gauge fixing recovers only \rhval{main/logreg-z2-fixed/ising/acc_far/mean:2}.
The FSS collapse did \emph{not} improve on the single-size crossing for the CNN (collapse error \rhval{main/cnn/ising/tc_error_fss/mean:3}, $p=\rhval{analysis/paired-tests/ising/h2_cnn_fss_vs_l32_p/mean:2}$) or the MLP (\rhval{main/mlp/ising/tc_error_fss/mean:3}), the CNN collapse exponent is biased ($\nu=\rhval{main/cnn/ising/nu_fss/mean:2}$ versus the exact 1), and the collapse of learning-by-confusion curves fails outright.
Reducing the training set to 10 samples per chain raises the MLP error beyond a factor of two even when the number of gradient updates is held fixed, whereas the CNN error does not change once the updates are matched. This is a small CPU study with three lattice sizes and reimplemented baselines.
