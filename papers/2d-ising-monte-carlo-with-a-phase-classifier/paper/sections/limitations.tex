
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scale.} Three small lattices ($L\le32$), one universality class, \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds in the main group and five in the sweeps; with ten seeds the smallest attainable two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ is $0.002$, and a collapse over three sizes is poorly conditioned (the CNN exponent runs into the search boundary). A full study would use more sizes, more seeds, and statistical errors on each curve.
\item \textbf{Baselines are reimplemented and not tuned.} Hyperparameters were fixed a priori; the confusion networks are small, the PCA read-out (peak of $\mathrm{Var}|z_1|$) and the collapse curves chosen for PCA and confusion are our choices, and other read-outs of those methods (e.g.\ thresholding the first component) may behave differently. The finding that confusion collapses fail concerns our collapse recipe, not learning by confusion in general.
\item \textbf{Prior knowledge in the protocol.} The training windows use the rounded $T_c^{\rm ref}$, the peak searches use $[1.9,3.1]$, and the confusion trial range is $[1.7,3.3]$; all of these encode that $T_c\approx2.3$. The main collapse window ($0.6$) makes $T_c$ infeasible for the confusion curves, see Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.
\item \textbf{Resolution.} Errors of $\approx0.02$ are well below the temperature spacing of \rhval{const/temp_step:3}; the crossings are interpolated, and the collapse grid step is \rhval{const/grid_resolution_tc:3}. The temperature grid has a node at $T=\rhval{const/tc_grid_node}$, which essentially coincides with the exact $T_c$; this can make argmax-plus-parabola and interpolated read-outs look better than they would on a grid offset from $T_c$, and we did not repeat the study on an offset grid. The sampler was checked against Onsager's energy at four temperatures, not against critical observables, and decorrelation was checked through the autocorrelation of $|m|$ at one size only.
\item \textbf{Mechanisms.} Only the symmetry explanation for the raw logistic regression is isolated by a control. Explanations for the size-independent bias of the CNN, for the failure of confusion collapses and for the biased CNN exponent are conjectures, not tested. The sweeps change one factor at a time and include only the MLP and CNN; the scaled-epoch training-size sweep matches the number of updates but not the learning-rate schedule or the total number of distinct configurations, and the remaining MLP sensitivity is not explained by it.
\item \textbf{Same-chain structure.} The held-out chain is independent of the training chains, but the four chains per temperature share the sampler and, per seed, the random stream; independent MC runs with different algorithms would be a stronger test.
\end{itemize}
