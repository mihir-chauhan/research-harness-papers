\textbf{H1: flow matching beats DDIM at four steps} --- \emph{partly supported; reversed on two moons}. At $K{=}4$ FM-Euler has lower SW than DDIM on eight Gaussians (Table~\ref{tab:sw}; $p<0.001$ in Table~\ref{tab:tests}) and on the checkerboard ($p<0.01$), but the checkerboard gap (0.013) is about one DDIM seed standard deviation, and on two moons DDIM has the lower \emph{SW} (0.203 against 0.263 for FM, $p<0.01$; the same direction nominally at $K{=}2$, 8 and 16, where the $K{=}16$ gap is small). This reversal is specific to SW: the MMD at $K{=}4$ on the same runs favours FM on every task, including two moons (0.0145 against 0.0252, Table~\ref{tab:mmd}; $p\le0.001$ on each task), so by MMD H1 holds on all three tasks and the two-moons failure is metric-dependent. The registered refutation rule (DDIM not worse on at least two tasks) is not triggered, but ``flow matching gives better few-step samples'' is not a general statement in this study. At $K{=}1$ FM has the lower SW on all tasks, yet its MMD is higher than that of DDIM on all three, so the two metrics disagree there; we have not isolated why. DDPM ancestral and DDIM coincide at $K{=}1$ (the last step has no noise). At $K{=}4$ ancestral sampling is worse than DDIM in SW and MMD on two moons and the checkerboard ($p<0.001$); on eight Gaussians both differences are within seed noise (SW 0.329 $\pm$ 0.015 against 0.288 $\pm$ 0.043; the MMD gap of 0.0087 is about one DDIM seed standard deviation).

\textbf{H2: the gap closes with more steps} --- \emph{nominally refuted; mixed}. The SW ratio DDIM/FM (Table~\ref{tab:tests}) moves towards 1 between $K{=}4$ and $K{=}100$ only on eight Gaussians; on the checkerboard it moves away from 1, and on two moons it changes from DDIM-better to FM-better, with $|r-1|$ (the rule as registered) nominally larger at $K{=}100$ (0.77 to 1.25), although a log-ratio would call it slightly closer. At $K{=}100$ FM has the lower mean SW on all tasks, but the evidence differs by task. On eight Gaussians DDIM's seed-to-seed spread is about five times that of FM (0.057 $\pm$ 0.028 against 0.027 $\pm$ 0.005) and the gap is not significant ($p=0.086$); on two moons the spreads are similar and the gap of 0.006 is marginal ($p=0.048$); on the checkerboard the gap is consistent across seeds (0.053 $\pm$ 0.002 against 0.031 $\pm$ 0.003, $p<0.001$), so there DDIM has not reached the FM error by 100 steps.

\textbf{H3: one reflow round} --- \emph{supported}. Reflow-1 lowers one-step SW on every task by a large margin (for example 0.042 against 1.234 on eight Gaussians, $p<0.001$) and is within 0.025 SW of the real-data floor at every $K$ of Table~\ref{tab:sw} on every task, while its MMD stays above the floor (0.0016 against 0.0005 on the checkerboard at $K{=}4$). At $K{=}100$ it does not improve: the difference to FM-Euler is at most 0.0012 and slightly \emph{positive} (worse) with $p<0.05$ on all three tasks. Reflow-1 received twice the updates of FM-Euler plus 20000 teacher trajectories (no FM control).

\textbf{H4: straightness} --- \emph{first part refuted, second part supported}. By chord/arc length FM paths (0.6565, 0.6763, 0.4500 for the three tasks) are \emph{less} straight than DDIM paths (0.7524, 0.8142, 0.7770; $p<0.001$), while Reflow-1 paths are at 0.9988 or more. FM is less straight than DDIM on all three tasks, yet has lower SW at $K{=}4$ on two of them and lower MMD on all three, so straightness by this measure does not order the two families by few-step quality. Among the Euler-sampled flow models of Table~\ref{tab:sw}, the straighter one (Reflow-1) is the better model at $K\le4$; the 4-step-teacher student of Sec.~\ref{sec:ablations} shows that the converse does not hold. The measure compares trajectories of different parametrisations (a DDIM path in $x$-space includes its $\sqrt{\bar\alpha}$ rescaling), so this refutation is specific to the measure we registered.

\textbf{Heun at equal evaluations (exploratory).} FM-Heun has the lowest SW in Table~\ref{tab:sw} among the systems without a second training stage at every $K$ from 1 to 16, but it uses $2K$ network evaluations, so it must be compared with the $2K$ columns of the other samplers (Table~\ref{tab:tests}). At 8 evaluations Heun ($K{=}4$) has lower SW than FM-Euler ($K{=}8$) on two moons (0.041 against 0.125) and the checkerboard (0.039 against 0.113), both $p<0.001$, and by a small margin on eight Gaussians (0.046 against 0.054, $p=0.034$); it is below DDIM at 8 evaluations on two moons and the checkerboard, and not significantly on eight Gaussians ($p=0.066$). The advantage disappears at low budgets: at 4 evaluations Heun is worse than Euler on eight Gaussians (0.138 against 0.086), and at 2 evaluations it is worse on all three tasks. From 8 evaluations on, a second-order solver on the unchanged flow model thus closes most of the gap to Reflow-1 (0.041 against 0.031 for Reflow-1 at $K{=}8$ on two moons) without reflow's extra training. We ran no second-order solver for the diffusion model, so this is not a statement about flows against diffusion.
