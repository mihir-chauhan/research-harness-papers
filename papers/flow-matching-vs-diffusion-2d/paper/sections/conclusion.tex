With a shared MLP and no tuning, flow matching has lower SW than DDIM at one sampling step on three 2D densities but higher one-step MMD; at four steps it has lower SW on eight Gaussians, marginally lower SW on the checkerboard, and higher SW on two moons, while MMD ranks it ahead on all three tasks; the change of its advantage with the step count is mixed (the DDIM/FM SW ratio shrinks on eight Gaussians, grows on the checkerboard and changes from DDIM-better to FM-better on two moons), and by chord/arc length its paths are less straight than DDIM paths. One reflow round gives near-floor quality at one step, no gain at 100 steps, and a quality ceiling set by the teacher's own sampler, with a second round adding little; without any retraining, Heun on the flow-matching model has lower SW than Euler at 8 network evaluations on all three tasks (by a small margin on eight Gaussians) and higher SW at 2. These are small-scale, five-seed observations; a full study would add tuned diffusion samplers, equal-compute reflow controls, more seeds and image-scale models.
