# Landscape
Behler-Parrinello (2007) introduced neural network potentials built from atom-centred symmetry functions with atomwise sum pooling; Behler (2011) details the functions. Gaussian-process/kernel approaches use similarity of atomic environments (Bartok et al., SOAP). Rupp et al. use the sorted eigenvalue spectrum of the Coulomb matrix to remove ordering dependence. Barthelme et al. (2022) analyse Gaussian-kernel regression in the flat limit (very wide kernels), where it tends to polynomial regression and is numerically hard to reach; our symmetry-function KRR selects such kernels. Deep Sets gives the sum-pooling characterisation of permutation-invariant functions. SchNet and NequIP learn invariant/equivariant features end to end. Comparisons of fingerprints and potentials exist (Zuo et al. 2019; Parsaeifard et al. 2020) and a review by Unke et al. LJ clusters have a well-known landscape (Wales and Doye 1997).
Gap: a tiny controlled study isolating descriptor invariance vs regressor on one analytic system, with measured rotation/permutation failure and learning curves. This is a replication-scale study, not a new method.
