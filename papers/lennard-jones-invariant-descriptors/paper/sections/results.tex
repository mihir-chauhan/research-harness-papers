\textbf{Overview.} Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the results at $n{=}500$. Sorted-distance MLP is best (0.635), followed by our pre-registered SymFn-sum KRR (0.889), sorted-distance KRR (1.095), the atomwise BP-style network (3.130), SymFn-sum MLP (4.513), and the raw-coordinate models (12.178 KRR, 15.028 MLP) against 12.203 for the mean predictor. Over 5 seeds SymFn-sum KRR is worse than the sorted-distance MLP (Welch $p=0.014$, Table~\ref{tab:tests} and \texttt{rh compare}) and better than sorted-distance KRR ($p=0.026$); its seed standard deviation (0.140) exceeds that of both sorted-distance systems.

\textbf{H1 (invariant descriptors beat raw): supported.} The four registered comparisons at $n{=}500$ all have $p<0.001$ (Table~\ref{tab:tests}), with large margins (the closest is SymFn-sum MLP 4.513 versus raw MLP 15.028). The same direction holds at the other sizes, where only SymFn-sum MLP versus raw MLP at $n{=}50$ is unresolved ($p=0.311$). The comparison is partly trivial, however: raw KRR is not distinguishable from the mean predictor at any size ($p\ge0.062$; 12.178 versus 12.203 at $n{=}500$, 11.14 versus 11.80 at $n{=}1500$), and the raw MLP is worse than the mean predictor at every size ($p\le0.031$).

\textbf{H2 (rotation/permutation behaviour): split verdict.} Invariant descriptors are unchanged: the rot+perm column equals the clean MAE for all sorted-distance and SF systems, and the largest per-seed invariance defect of any invariant system is $8.34\cdot10^{-5}$ (SymFn-sum KRR), below the registered $10^{-4}$ but not by a wide margin. The defect is exactly 0 for the sum-pooled SF MLP and the sorted-distance MLP, $5.39\cdot10^{-7}$ at most for the atomwise MLP and $1.19\cdot10^{-10}$ for sorted-distance KRR. The SymFn-sum KRR value is a mean over test configurations; single configurations change by up to $4.2\cdot10^{-3}$ in the main runs so this model is invariant only up to an error that grows as the selected kernel becomes flatter (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). The ``degrades at least $2\times$'' prediction for raw models is \emph{refuted}: raw KRR goes from 12.178 to 12.143 and raw MLP from 15.028 to 14.918 in Table~\ref{tab:main}, which is expected for models that are no better than a constant. What the raw models do show is a large prediction inconsistency: their defect is 2.88 (KRR) and 15.2 (MLP).

\textbf{H3 (SF-KRR beats sorted-distance KRR at small $n$): supported, with caveats.} At $n{=}50$ SF-KRR has MAE 2.65 versus 3.93 for sorted KRR ($p=0.012$) and at $n{=}150$ 1.49 versus 2.07 ($p=0.032$; Tables~\ref{tab:lc} and~\ref{tab:tests}), so both registered tests pass at $\alpha=0.05$. Three caveats limit this. First, each test has 3 seeds and the two tests are uncorrected; the $n{=}150$ test would not pass a Bonferroni threshold of 0.025. Second, the advantage does not persist: it is still present at $n{=}500$ ($p=0.026$) but reversed at $n{=}1500$, where sorted KRR reaches 0.45 and SF-KRR 0.71 ($p=0.022$). Third, the verdict depends on the hyperparameter grid: with the narrow grid of our first version SF-KRR is worse than sorted KRR at $n{=}500$ (1.587 versus 1.096, Table~\ref{tab:grid}), and that version reported H3 as not supported. H3 concerns KRR only; the sorted-distance MLP (2.39 and 1.21 at $n{=}50$ and 150) has a lower mean error than SF-KRR at both sizes (not resolved, $p=0.359$ and 0.125), so the data do not show that symmetry functions are the more data-efficient descriptor in general.

\textbf{H4 (KRR beats MLP at small $n$): mixed, descriptor dependent.} For raw input KRR is lower at every size ($p\le0.034$), but KRR is at the level of the mean predictor and the MLP is above it, so this is not informative. For SF-sum input KRR is lower at every size, with $p\le0.012$ for $n\ge150$; at $n{=}50$ the MLP has one poor seed (std 6.38) and the difference is not resolved ($p=0.147$). For sorted distances the \emph{MLP} is better at $n{=}50,150,500$ ($p\le0.009$) and the two tie at $n{=}1500$ (0.45 versus 0.45, $p=0.977$): the gap shrinks with data as H4 states, but its sign at small $n$ is opposite for this descriptor. By the registered majority criterion (two of three descriptors have KRR ahead at $n\le150$) H4 is nominally supported, which we regard as weak, since one of the two is the uninformative raw case, and for SF input the MLP error remains several times the KRR error at every size (Table~\ref{tab:lc}).

\textbf{Where it fails.} (i) The two networks on symmetry functions are the clearest failure: SymFn-sum MLP has 4.513 at $n{=}500$ and 4.13 at $n{=}1500$, and the atomwise network 3.130 and 2.67, while plain linear ridge regression on the same pooled descriptor reaches 0.805 at $n{=}500$ (Table~\ref{tab:abl}). The descriptor therefore carries the information and our fixed network training does not extract it. Both networks are worse on random than on perturbed-minimum configurations (5.387 versus 3.639 and 3.655 versus 2.605 in Table~\ref{tab:main}), unlike the sorted-distance MLP (0.668 versus 0.601). (ii) SF-KRR saturates: it improves from 0.89 to 0.71 between $n{=}500$ and $1500$ while sorted KRR improves from 1.09 to 0.45, so with the most data it is the least accurate of the three accurate systems. A limited resolution of the 21 pooled functions may be the cause; we did not vary the number of functions. (iii) We did not examine per-size errors, so we cannot say whether any $N$ dominates.
