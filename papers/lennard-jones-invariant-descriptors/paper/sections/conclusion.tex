On small LJ clusters, an invariant descriptor is necessary for a useful model in our setup: raw coordinates give errors at or above the mean-predictor level, and rotation or permutation then changes their predictions by \rhval{main/raw-coords-krr/lj_clusters/inv_defect/mean} to \rhval{main/raw-coords-mlp/lj_clusters/inv_defect/mean} energy units on average. Among invariant systems at $n{=}500$, the sorted-distance MLP was the most accurate (0.635), symmetry-function KRR second (0.889) and sorted-distance KRR third (1.095). Symmetry-function KRR was ahead of sorted-distance KRR for $n\le500$ and behind it at $n{=}1500$, which supports the pre-registered small-data hypothesis for KRR on few seeds; the KRR-versus-MLP ranking depends on the descriptor. The symmetry-function result needed a much wider kernel-width range than the sorted-distance one, the selected kernels were nearly flat, linear ridge on the same descriptor did as well (0.805), and angular functions had no resolved effect (0.785 without them versus 0.889 with them). A shared hyperparameter grid that was adequate for one descriptor reversed the ranking of another. These findings come from one toy system, few seeds and reimplemented baselines, and are not evidence about production potentials.
