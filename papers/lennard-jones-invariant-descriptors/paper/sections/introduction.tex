Machine-learned interatomic potentials replace expensive quantum-mechanical energy evaluations by a regression model of the potential energy surface~\cite{unke2020machine}. The true energy is invariant under rotation, translation and permutation of identical atoms, so the choice of input representation decides whether a model must learn these symmetries from data or has them by construction. Two families dominate: atom-centred symmetry functions fed to atomwise neural networks~\cite{behler2007generalized,behler2011atom} and kernel methods on descriptors of atomic environments~\cite{bartk2012representing}; earlier work on molecules used the sorted eigenvalue spectrum of the Coulomb matrix to remove the atom-ordering dependence~\cite{rupp2011fast}. Learned invariant and equivariant message-passing networks have since become standard~\cite{schtt2017schnet,batzner2021e}.

We ask a deliberately small question on a system with an analytic reference: for Lennard-Jones (LJ) clusters of 5--13 atoms, how do (a) raw coordinates, (b) sorted pair distances and (c) sum-pooled symmetry functions compare when combined with kernel ridge regression (KRR) or a small multilayer perceptron (MLP), in terms of energy error, data efficiency and behaviour when the test set is rotated or its atoms are permuted? We pre-registered four hypotheses (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The study is a reimplementation-scale comparison on CPU; we claim no new method.

Contributions: (i) a controlled comparison with identical data, splits and tuning budget for all seven systems and a mean-predictor reference; (ii) learning curves from 50 to 1500 training configurations; (iii) measured invariance defects under rotation and permutation; (iv) three ablations (angular functions, linear ridge instead of the kernel, training-time augmentation) and two sensitivity checks (KRR hyperparameter grid, MLP training length). The outcome is mixed: our pre-registered method, symmetry-function KRR, is second to the sorted-distance MLP at $n{=}500$, raw coordinates give no useful model, and the ranking of symmetry-function KRR against sorted-distance KRR depends on the width of the hyperparameter grid (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), which the first version of this study got wrong.
