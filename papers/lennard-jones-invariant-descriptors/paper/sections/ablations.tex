\textbf{KRR hyperparameter grid.} Table~\ref{tab:grid} repeats the two invariant KRR systems with three grids. Sorted-distance KRR is insensitive (1.096, 1.095, 1.095). SymFn-sum KRR is not: 1.587 with the narrow grid, where 4 of 5 seeds select the smallest $\gamma$, against 0.907 and 0.889 with the mid and wide grids ($p<0.001$ narrow versus wide, $p=0.824$ mid versus wide, Welch). The narrow grid was therefore unfair to this one system; the wide grid is used everywhere else. With the wide grid the selected $\gamma$ is interior in all but one run (Table~\ref{tab:hp}; SF-KRR, $n{=}50$, one seed at $10^{-7}$). The selected $\lambda$ is a different matter: SymFn-sum KRR takes the smallest value whose solve is not flagged as ill-conditioned in 13 of its 14 runs, and sorted-distance KRR in 4 of 14. We could not bracket it: smaller values give solves that LAPACK flags as ill-conditioned. The energies are noise-free, so the validation error may keep falling as $\lambda\to0$ until round-off stops it. Two observations bound the consequence at $n{=}500$: widening from mid to wide changes the error only from 0.907 to 0.889, and linear ridge, whose $\lambda$ is interior in all 5 seeds, gives a similar error (next paragraph). The price of the flat kernels is numerical: the invariance defect of SymFn-sum KRR rises from $1.13\cdot10^{-6}$ to $1.58\cdot10^{-5}$ to $6.16\cdot10^{-5}$ across the three grids, which may be round-off in the descriptor amplified by large dual coefficients; we did not measure the coefficients.

\textbf{Linear ridge instead of the kernel.} For SymFn-sum input, ridge regression without a kernel gives 0.805, not distinguishable from RBF KRR (0.889, $p=0.269$; Table~\ref{tab:abl}); for sorted distances it gives 5.647 against 1.095. Together with $\gamma\le10^{-3}$ in every SF-KRR run, this suggests that the selected SF kernels act almost linearly on the pooled descriptor, the flat-limit behaviour analysed in~\cite{barthelm2022gaussian}. This is plausible for a pair potential, since $E$ and the pooled radial functions are both sums over pairs of a function of $r_{ij}$, but we did not test other potentials.

\textbf{Angular symmetry functions.} Dropping the eight angular functions gives 0.785 for KRR against 0.889 with them ($p=0.190$), 0.914 for linear ridge, and leaves the MLP unchanged within noise (4.582 versus 4.610, $p=0.830$). No effect of the angular terms is resolved in either direction. The first version of this study, with the narrow grid, reported a gain from removing them for KRR, which the wide grid does not reproduce.

\textbf{Augmentation for raw coordinates.} Training the raw MLP on freshly rotated and permuted minibatches lowers the MAE from 15.090 to 9.773 ($p<0.001$), still far above any invariant descriptor, and the invariance defect drops from 15.2 to 1.7 but is not removed (Table~\ref{tab:abl}). At this budget, augmentation is much less effective than building the symmetry into the descriptor.

\textbf{MLP training length.} Because the MLPs may be undertrained, we varied the step budget (Table~\ref{tab:steps}). Sorted-distance MLP improves from 0.89 to 0.65 to 0.54 over 1000, 3000 and 12000 steps ($p=0.003$ and 0.006 against 3000 steps), so it was not converged at the budget of the main runs. SymFn-sum MLP goes from 4.81 to 4.61 to 4.39, differences that are not resolved with 3 seeds ($p=0.264$ and 0.056). Even with 4$\times$ more steps the SF-sum MLP (4.39) stays far from SF-KRR and linear ridge, so its gap is not closed by the step count within this range.
