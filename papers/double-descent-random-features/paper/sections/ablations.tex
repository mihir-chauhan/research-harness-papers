The ablation compares tuning protocols for the ridge (Table~\ref{tab:h4}, columns Tuned, Global, LOO, val50), and the ridge sweep of Section~\ref{sec:results} is the sensitivity study.

\textbf{H5 (other protocols also remove the hump; exploratory) -- mixed.} GlobalRidge has a mean hump below $0.05$ on all tasks, and below that of TunedRidge on seven of eight tasks (digits\_n0 is the exception, 0.0005 against 0.0000), so on these tasks a single $\lambda$ shared across widths is enough and per-width tuning brings no visible benefit; the final errors of the two are nearly identical (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Inside the threshold window $0.4\le N/n\le4$ the mean selected $\log_{10}\lambda$ moves by only 0.1--0.9 dex (Fig.~\ref{fig:lam}), which may be why per-width selection adds little there; across the full grid it varies by 2.3--2.7 dex on five of the eight tasks (e.g.\ synth\_n0, from $-1.0$ at $N/n=0.05$ to $-3.7$ at $N/n=25$), and we did not isolate why per-width selection does not help. Tuning on 50 validation points (val50) gives a mean hump above the threshold on synth\_n0.25 (0.0501, essentially at it) and synth\_n1 (0.1555), and leave-one-out tuning on synth\_n1 (0.0600); synth\_n1 is the noisiest task, while synth\_n0.25 is only at the threshold with a large seed sd (0.089); that selecting among 15 values from few or noisy labels is less reliable is a possible mechanism we did not test. Their variance across seeds is large (Table~\ref{tab:h4} gives means only; the per-seed values are in \texttt{results/analysis.md}), so with 5 seeds the ranking among TunedRidge, GlobalRidge and LOO is not resolved.

\textbf{Where the method fails.} (a) At zero or small noise on digits, tuned and untuned interpolation reach the same final error (Table~\ref{tab:main}, digits\_n0), so tuning only removes the peak, it does not improve the large-width solution. (b) A large fixed ridge has no peak but a high, flat curve (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}, $\lambda\ge10^{-1}$ gives larger window maxima than $10^{-3}$). (c) On individual seeds the tuned hump reaches $0.06$ of the best error, which is small in absolute terms but not zero; the hump statistic is relative to the best error and small values on a flat curve are inside the noise of the validation selection.
