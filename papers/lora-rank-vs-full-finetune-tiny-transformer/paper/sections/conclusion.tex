In a 70k-parameter transformer, LoRA at rank 4 on all linear layers matched full fine-tuning on two adaptation tasks with \rhval{cmp/main/lora-r-4/sort_desc/trainable_params/inv_ratio:pct1}\% of the parameters, but a from-scratch model matched both at 2000 examples, so the tasks are easy at that data size. Accuracy rose with rank on descending sort (0.978 to 0.997), while on reversal whole-run optimisation failures at ranks 1, 2 and 8 hide any rank trend. Last-block tuning was worse than LoRA on descending sort and head-only tuning failed. Every adapted system lost the pretraining task almost completely, so the forgetting hypothesis is not supported and, because retention is at floor even for head-only tuning, the test is uninformative. In the low-data runs, LoRA rank 4 had the highest mean at 300 examples, but this is a three-seed observation without a significance test, at a full fine-tuning learning rate tuned for 2000 examples, and we draw no conclusion about LoRA versus full fine-tuning at small $N$. The code, run registry and tables reproduce all numbers.
