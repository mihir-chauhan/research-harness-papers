The model has 70k parameters, tasks are two synthetic permutations of eight digits, and both are close to the ceiling at $N=2000$, so the main comparison has little resolving power; the interesting differences appear only in subsampled or failing regimes, where learning rates were not retuned per $N$ and the LoRA versus full fine-tuning ordering depends on that choice. Five seeds (three for the sweeps) give paired tests with little power, and the tests are not corrected for multiple comparisons. Every baseline is reimplemented here, with one learning rate per system chosen on one validation seed from a coarse grid; after one extension the selected rates for full fine-tuning, LoRA, last block and head are still at the upper grid edge with validation accuracy not yet saturated, so every system except scratch may be under-tuned, and the comparison is only as fair as this one-validation-seed tuning; the LoRA rate was tuned at rank 4 and reused for other ranks, which may explain the failures of ranks 1, 2 and 8 on reversal, and we did not test that explanation. Only the learning rate was tuned: the LoRA scale $\alpha/r$, initialisation, number of steps and layer selection of the last-block baseline were fixed a priori. The pretrained model saw a single task token and was never trained to condition on it, and retention is at floor for every system including head-only tuning, so the forgetting comparison has no resolving power. Total forgetting may follow from our choices (1500 steps, no replay, fixed random embedding for the new task token, adapters active for every input) and says little about large models; our result does not contradict findings at scale~\cite{biderman2024lora}. We did not measure forgetting under early stopping or with a partial-learning setup, and no other PEFT variants (adapters, BitFit, DoRA) were run. A full study would add larger models and natural-language data, more seeds, per-method and per-rank learning-rate tuning, and mechanism experiments (singular-value spectra of the full fine-tuning update, rank-wise learning-rate sweeps) to explain the failures.
