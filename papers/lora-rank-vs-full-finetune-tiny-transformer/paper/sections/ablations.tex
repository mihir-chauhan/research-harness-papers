\textbf{Target modules.} Table~\ref{tab:targets} compares LoRA on all six linear maps with LoRA on $\{q,v\}$ only. At rank 1, $\{q,v\}$ (512 parameters) reaches 0.577 on descending sort and 0.056 on reversal, against 0.978 and 0.799 for all-linear (1,792 parameters). At rank 4, $\{q,v\}$ (2,048 parameters) reaches 0.971 and 1.000 against 0.997 and 0.999. At rank 4 the two settings also differ in parameters (2,048 against 7,168), so this ablation changes the parameter count together with the targets and does not separate the two. It does show that, at tiny rank, restricting LoRA to $\{q,v\}$ (a choice common in the LoRA literature) reaches lower accuracy than all-linear in this setting.

\textbf{Adaptation set size.} Table~\ref{tab:ntrain} (descending sort, seeds 0--2, same learning rates) examines low-data behaviour. With $N=300$ the means are 0.765 (full fine-tuning), 0.511 (scratch), 0.838, 0.938 and 0.621 (LoRA $r=1,4,8$); with $N=100$ they are 0.204, 0.116, 0.385, 0.337 and 0.000. Pretrained full fine-tuning is ahead of scratch at both sizes, so pretraining is useful when data are scarce, in this three-seed sample. LoRA at ranks 1 and 4 has higher means than full fine-tuning at $N=300$ and $N=100$, but we report no test that supports an ordering: standard deviations at $N=100$ (\rhval{sweep_ntrain/lora-r-4@n_train=100/sort_desc/adapt_acc/std:2}--\rhval{sweep_ntrain/lora-r-1@n_train=100/sort_desc/adapt_acc/std:2}) are as large as the gaps, \texttt{rh compare} does not pair runs within one value of $N$, so no significance test is given for the comparisons of LoRA $r=1,4$ with full fine-tuning, and the full fine-tuning rate ($10^{-2}$) was tuned for $N=2000$ and may be too high for small $N$, so the comparison may favour LoRA unfairly. LoRA $r=4$ at $N=300$ (0.938, std 0.007) has the largest gap in the means, again without a test. Rank 8 is at or below \rhval{sweep_ntrain/lora-r-8@n_train=100/sort_desc/adapt_acc/max:4} in all three seeds at $N=100$ and at \rhval{sweep_ntrain/lora-r-8@n_train=300/sort_desc/adapt_acc/min:3} in one of three seeds at $N=300$ (std \rhval{sweep_ntrain/lora-r-8@n_train=300/sort_desc/adapt_acc/std:3}). In these failed runs the final training loss stayed high, so they look like optimisation failures at the shared high learning rate and not memorisation; we did not test whether a lower rate repairs them. Learning rates were not retuned per $N$.
