On 8$\times$8 digits with a 41k-parameter CNN trained to the final epoch, plain CE memorised essentially all corrupted labels and fell from 0.991 to 0.709 test accuracy at 40\% symmetric noise; label smoothing did not help at the final epoch and was slightly worse at 40\% (it exceeded CE at earlier logged epochs); mixup ($\alpha{=}1$) helped modestly while still memorising about 90\% of corrupted labels; and small-loss selection with the true noise rate was clearly best (0.896 vs 0.709 for CE at 40\%) and the only method to classify most corrupted training examples by their true class. The selection rule's success depends on the assumed forget rate (under-estimation is costly, over-estimation to 0.6 was not), and larger mixup $\alpha$ gave progressively better accuracy, so the pre-set $\alpha{=}1$ understates mixup. These results are from one small dataset, five seeds and untuned baselines; a fuller study would use larger datasets, deeper networks, tuned and equally budgeted baselines with a noisy validation set, estimated noise rates, and non-symmetric noise.
