In this small study, SINDy-STLSQ with a library containing the true terms predicted multi-step trajectories better than an MLP neural ODE and DMDc on the pendulum in every setting tested and on Van der Pol up to noise 0.05 and from 500 transitions upward. It also produced divergent models on Van der Pol, in one of five seeds at noise 0.1 and in two of five at 200 transitions, where the small-data part of H1 is refuted. The absolute advantage over the MLP grew with noise up to 0.05 on both systems (up to 0.1 on Van der Pol) instead of shrinking. LQR cost was far less sensitive to the model than prediction error on Van der Pol, while on the pendulum the linear model failed and the MLP degraded with noise and was very poor at 200 transitions under our fixed training budget. These conclusions are limited by scale, untuned reimplemented baselines and a favourable library.
