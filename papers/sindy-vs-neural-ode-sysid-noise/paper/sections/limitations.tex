This is a small CPU study. It covers two two-dimensional systems with a scalar input, full-state measurements with additive Gaussian noise, no process noise, and five seeds; the $p$-values are uncorrected, and a single divergent seed changes a mean-based verdict. Data size was varied at one noise level only, and noise at one data size only. The MLP's budget is a fixed \rhval{const/mlp_gradient_steps} gradient steps at every data size; we did not sweep it, so its small-data results (200 and 500 transitions) depend on that choice. A first version of the code regressed the SINDy derivative on $u_k$ instead of the stencil average and trained the MLP for a fixed number of epochs; both flaws were fixed, all reported numbers come from the rerun, and the earlier runs are archived under \texttt{results/archive\_v1}. All baselines are reimplemented, use one fixed hyperparameter setting and were not tuned; the MLP might look different with multi-step or noise-aware training, and we did not test whether ensemble~\cite{fasel2022ensemble} or weak-form~\cite{messenger2020weak} SINDy removes the divergent SINDy seeds. The size of a divergent prediction error depends on the clipping bound of the metric. Closed-loop tasks are local regulation from small initial conditions; MPC is a short-horizon sampling planner whose own suboptimality is larger than the differences between models on Van der Pol. The pendulum library contains the exact trig terms and the Van der Pol library the exact monomials, which favours SINDy; the library ablation shows how much. A full study would add more systems and dimensions, a tuning budget per method, a noise $\times$ size grid, process noise, output feedback, and tracking tasks.
