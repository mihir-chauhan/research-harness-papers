Model-based control needs a model, and in practice that model is identified from noisy measurements. Two widely used families of data-driven models are sparse regression on a library of candidate terms, as in SINDy~\cite{brunton2016discovering,brunton2016sparse}, which returns an interpretable ODE, and flexible neural-network dynamics~\cite{chen2018neural,nagabandi2018neural}, which trade interpretability for expressiveness. Linear least-squares models in the DMD-with-control tradition~\cite{proctor2016dynamic} are a simpler alternative. Which family to prefer depends on the noise level, the amount of data, and, importantly, on what the model is used for: a model with small trajectory error need not give a better controller, and the controller may be insensitive to model errors away from the operating point.

This paper is a controlled, small-scale comparison, not a new method. We ask how SINDy-STLSQ, an MLP neural ODE and DMDc compare in (i) open-loop multi-step prediction error and (ii) closed-loop cost of LQR and MPC controllers designed on the identified model, on an inverted damped pendulum and the Van der Pol oscillator, as measurement noise and data quantity vary. We state three hypotheses with refutation criteria fixed in advance (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) and report which are supported.

We contribute a reproducible numpy/PyTorch implementation with a run registry behind every number, two one-dimensional sweeps (noise at fixed data size, data size at fixed noise) with 5 seeds, an ablation and sensitivity study of the SINDy library, smoothing and threshold, and an account of where the comparison is inconclusive or goes against SINDy.
