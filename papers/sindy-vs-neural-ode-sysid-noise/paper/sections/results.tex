\textbf{H1, noise sweep.} On the pendulum SINDy has the lowest prediction error among the three identified models at every noise level in Table~\ref{tab:main}; it is better than the MLP and DMDc in 5 of 5 seeds, with paired $p\le0.015$, at every noise level (Table~\ref{tab:gap}). DMDc is near 1.0, i.e.\ no better than predicting the mean. On Van der Pol, SINDy is best up to noise $0.05$ (5 of 5 seeds). At noise $0.1$ the SINDy mean (0.389) is inflated by one divergent seed (seed 2, 1.748); Table~\ref{tab:vdpseeds} shows that the other four SINDy seeds are below every MLP and DMDc seed, and the SINDy median is 0.048 against 0.283 (MLP) and 0.386 (DMDc), but the paired tests are not significant ($p=0.819$ and $0.998$). At noise 0, the level named in the refutation criterion, SINDy is better in 5 of 5 seeds on both systems. This part of H1 is therefore supported for the pendulum at all noise levels and for Van der Pol up to noise $0.05$; at Van der Pol noise $0.1$ it is inconclusive.

The clause ``the gap shrinks as noise grows'' is not supported in absolute terms: in Table~\ref{tab:gap} the median MLP$-$SINDy difference grows from noise 0 to 0.05 on both systems (and from 0 to 0.1 on Van der Pol); on the pendulum it falls between 0.05 and 0.1 (0.478 to 0.341), where both models are already poor. In relative terms the advantage does shrink (pendulum medians: 0.007 versus 0.118 at noise 0, 0.471 versus 0.893 at noise 0.1). The recovered support degrades with noise (support F1 falls from 1.000 on the pendulum and 0.900 on Van der Pol at noise 0 to 0.323 and 0.435 at noise 0.1, with 21 and 18 non-zero terms on average). Noise amplification by central differences may be the cause; we did not isolate it, and smoothing helped on the pendulum but hurt on Van der Pol (Table~\ref{tab:abl}).

\textbf{H1, data size.} Table~\ref{tab:ntrain} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ntrain} show the size sweep at noise 0.05 (the only noise level at which size was varied). The mean prediction error of SINDy decreases with more data on both systems. The MLP error also falls with data but not monotonically on the pendulum (0.535 at 2000, 0.582 at 5000), and DMDc changes little. On the pendulum SINDy is below the MLP in 5 of 5 seeds at every size, including $N=200$ ($p=0.003$). On Van der Pol at $N=200$, however, two of the five SINDy seeds diverge in open-loop prediction (seeds 1 and 4: 4.965 and 15.425, Table~\ref{tab:vdpseeds}); SINDy has the lower error than the MLP in only 3 of 5 seeds (and than DMDc in 3 of 5), its mean (4.190) is far above the MLP's (0.333), the medians (0.289 and 0.281) do not separate the two, and the paired test gives $p=0.262$. By the registered criterion the small-data clause of H1 is therefore refuted on Van der Pol and supported on the pendulum; SINDy's advantage on Van der Pol holds from 500 transitions upward (5 of 5 seeds, $p\le0.014$). With $N=200$ the data are only five trajectories for 26 library coefficients, which may explain the unstable SINDy models, but we did not isolate this. The divergence does not carry over to the LQR controller: SINDy's LQR cost on Van der Pol at $N=200$ is 0.700 (Table~\ref{tab:ntrainlqr}).

\textbf{H2 (closed-loop cost).} On Van der Pol every model gives an LQR cost close to that of the true-model controller (Table~\ref{tab:main}) with zero failures, even where prediction error differs by a factor of several. Table~\ref{tab:paired} shows that the paired differences are small but not zero: DMDc is worse than the oracle in 5 of 5 seeds at every noise level, by 1.9--3.5\%; the MLP is worse by at most 0.4\% up to noise 0.05 and by 6.7\% at 0.1; SINDy is within 0.2\%. The MPC costs on Van der Pol lie between 1.321 and 1.447 for all models against 1.395 for the oracle, and DMDc's mean is below the oracle's at every noise level; a linear model beating the true one suggests that the short-horizon sampling planner is itself suboptimal, so these MPC differences carry little information. The LQR design uses only the linearisation at the origin, an unstable focus for Van der Pol, which every model class, including the linear one, can represent. On the pendulum, closed-loop cost does track prediction error: SINDy is within 3\% of the oracle up to noise 0.05 (23.1\% at 0.1), the MLP degrades with noise (4.9\% at 0.02, 142.5\% at 0.05, 1010.4\% at 0.1), and DMDc fails in most episodes (\texttt{lqr\_fail} 0.800 to 0.840); its fit over the whole sampled range may be a poor local model at the unstable equilibrium, which we did not test separately. Under MPC the same ordering (SINDy, MLP, DMDc) holds on the pendulum at noise 0.02 and 0.05; at noise 0.1 the MLP (30.295 $\pm$ 30.687) and DMDc (27.028 $\pm$ 6.085) are both poor and not separable. By the registered criterion H2 is supported for Van der Pol (with small but systematic differences for DMDc) and refuted for the pendulum, where the ordering by cost follows the ordering by prediction error.

\textbf{Failure case: the MLP controller at small data.} Table~\ref{tab:ntrainlqr} shows the closed-loop effect of data size. On the pendulum the MLP controller is very poor at 200 transitions (mean 430.585, median 121.376) and heavy-tailed at 500 (mean 208.285, median 1.616); from 1000 transitions upward its mean cost is between 2.446 and 2.564, more than twice SINDy's (1.075 to 1.095). SINDy's cost is 1.321 at 200, and DMDc stays between 30.877 and 35.807. Three MLP runs have an LQR cost of exactly 1000, the cap. We re-fitted these three models in registered diagnostic runs (Table~\ref{tab:diag}): the DARE was solved in all three, all 10 episodes were stopped at the cap, and the identified input gain at the origin has the wrong sign ($\hat B_2<0$ against $B_2=0.050$), so the gain designed on the model is destabilising on the true linearisation (spectral radius $\rho>1$). This failure is measured with a fixed, untuned budget of 2500 gradient steps, so it is a property of this training setup, not of neural models in general. On Van der Pol the LQR cost is between 0.687 and 0.718 for every model and size.
