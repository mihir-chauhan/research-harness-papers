\textbf{Sparse identification.} SINDy~\cite{brunton2016discovering} regresses time derivatives on a library of candidate functions and enforces sparsity by sequential thresholding; SINDYc~\cite{brunton2016sparse} adds control inputs, and SINDy-MPC~\cite{kaiser2018sparse} already compares MPC on a sparse model with neural-network and linear models under limited, noisy data; this paper is a small-scale re-examination of that kind of comparison on two systems, adding closed-loop LQR and sweeps over noise and data size. Noise robustness has been addressed by ensembling~\cite{fasel2022ensemble} and by the weak (integral) formulation, which avoids differentiating noisy data and was introduced for ODEs~\cite{messenger2020weak} and later extended to PDEs~\cite{messenger2021weak}; PySINDy~\cite{kaptanoglu2022pysindy} is an open-source package that implements these variants. We use plain STLSQ with finite-difference derivatives (optionally Savitzky--Golay smoothed), so our results describe the baseline algorithm, not the ensemble or weak variants.

\textbf{Neural dynamics models.} Neural ODEs~\cite{chen2018neural} parameterise the vector field with a network; neural dynamics models were used for model-based control in~\cite{nagabandi2018neural}, and deep networks can learn Koopman embeddings~\cite{lusch2018deep}. DMD with control~\cite{proctor2016dynamic} fits a linear input--state model by least squares; we use its discrete-time form on state and input.
