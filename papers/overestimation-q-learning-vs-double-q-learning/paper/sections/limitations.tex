This is a small, CPU-only study and its scope is narrow. (i) One MDP family: random20 has 20 states, 4 actions and 3 successors per pair, with a fixed discount of 0.7 and reward means from a standard normal; the conclusions about its bias sign and about which method takes fewer suboptimal actions may change with different gap-to-noise ratios, branching or discount, and our $\sigma$ sweep already shows the action-quality ranking changing within this family. (ii) The random-MDP configuration (discount, episode length, random start states, 1{,}000 episodes) was fixed after a pilot on seed 0; a discount of 0.9 with the same budget was not converged, so our random20 results describe a discount of 0.7 and a finite budget, not the asymptotic behaviour. (iii) Hyper-parameters are not tuned for any method ($\alpha=0.1$, $\epsilon=0.1$ throughout); a fair per-method tuning could reorder Q-learning, Double and Maxmin on random20, and our step-size sweep covers only Q-learning and Double. Weighted Double ($c=1$) and Maxmin ($N=2$) use one fixed setting each. (iv) All baselines are our own reimplementations from the published descriptions; we did not compare against reference code. (v) Only 5 seeds are used for tests, each the mean over 1{,}000 runs; this makes the between-seed spread tiny and the $p$-values small, but it measures reproducibility over seeds of one generator, not generalisation to other MDP families. (vi) The mechanisms offered in Section~\ref{sec:ablations} (effective step size, adaptive sampling) are supported only by one-factor sweeps; we did not run an experiment that fixes the data (e.g.\ off-policy learning from a common fixed dataset) or that equalises the number of updates per table. (vii) The trap-MDP start-state bias is clipped at zero by construction, so it cannot register underestimation, and the signed $Q$-bias is dominated by the zero initialisation unless warm-started. (viii) The simultaneous-update ablation is an in-place sequential implementation whose small random20 deviation from Q-learning is attributed to update order without a dedicated test. (ix) Tabular results do not carry over to function approximation, where bias can compound through bootstrapping. A fuller study would add more MDP families, a discount and budget grid with convergence checks, per-method tuning, many more seeds, and function approximation.
