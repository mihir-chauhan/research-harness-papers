In this small study of neural Bellman-Ford with training on at most 8 nodes, no registered comparison at 64 nodes was statistically significant. On the registered primary metric (mean error on sparse 64-node graphs) the sum MPNN trained on the final distance alone was lowest and had no failing sparse seed, although one of its seeds gave a non-finite error on dense graphs. The aggregation-aligned max MPNN trained on the final distance alone had the lowest error in distribution and, by the post hoc median, at 64 nodes, but it failed on two of five seeds there. Step-wise supervision did not help under our fixed budget, and the sum MPNN with it gave non-finite or astronomically large errors on growing-degree graphs at 64 nodes. The flattened-adjacency MLP was poor at every size, including in distribution. For the two step-supervised systems (three seeds each), running more message-passing steps than $n-1$ at test time raised the median error, and the error of most individual models, so these processors mostly do not behave as stable fixed-point iterations; the final-only models were not swept. The intuition that alignment plus hints yields size generalisation was therefore not confirmed here; a larger and tuned study is needed before drawing a conclusion either way.
