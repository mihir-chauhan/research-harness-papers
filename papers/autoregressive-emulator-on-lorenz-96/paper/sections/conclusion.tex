In a controlled two-scale Lorenz-96 study with a small circular CNN, five seeds and reimplemented baselines, rollout training gave a modest, monotone improvement in medium-range skill that is clear only for $K{=}8$ (RMSE at lead 2: \rhval{main/cnn-k-1/l96/rmse_t20/mean:3} to \rhval{main/cnn-k-8/l96/rmse_t20/mean:3}) and costs one-step accuracy. We found no stability problem for one-step training, no measurable variance loss from multi-step training, and a negative effect of input noise. All emulators beat persistence, climatology and a linear stencil in ACC lead time, but beyond about 4.5 time units the CNN RMSE exceeds that of climatology. The claims are limited to this small setting; a stability benefit of rollout training, if it exists, needs a harder regime than the one studied here.
