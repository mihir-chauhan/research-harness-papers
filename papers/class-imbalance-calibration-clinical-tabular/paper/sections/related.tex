\textbf{Imbalance corrections.} SMOTE creates synthetic minority examples by interpolating between a minority point and one of its $k$ nearest minority neighbours~\cite{chawla2011smote}; the imbalanced-learn toolbox packages SMOTE and many variants~\cite{lemaitre2016imbalanced}. We reimplement SMOTE and ROS in NumPy.

\textbf{Effects on calibration.} Dal Pozzolo et al.\ analyse how undersampling biases posterior probabilities and propose a correction of the posterior~\cite{pozzolo2015calibrating}; our prior-shift offset (Section~\ref{sec:method}) applies the same prior-ratio argument to oversampling and reweighting, as an ablation. The three clinical studies above~\cite{carriero2024harms,andersen2026tipping,sirikul2026class} already cover several learners, event fractions and real datasets; ours is much smaller and only adds a threshold-moving arm and the prior-shift offset, on a small bundled dataset.

\textbf{Calibration metrics and learners.} Guo et al.\ document miscalibration of modern neural networks, find temperature scaling effective and use the binned expected calibration error~\cite{guo2017calibration}; we use the Brier score and the logistic recalibration slope and intercept instead, since binned error depends on the binning. Gradient boosting~\cite{friedman2001greedy} is the learner behind tools such as XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost}; we use scikit-learn's implementation~\cite{pedregosa2012scikit} with fixed settings.
