H4 as registered (corrected Brier within 0.005 of no correction) holds in 23 of 24 cells; the exception is GB with SMOTE at 1:5, where the gap is $-0.0080$ (\emph{better} than no correction, as is the raw model at $-0.0073$). The criterion is weak: the raw gaps are already below 0.005 in 22 of 24 cells (the exceptions are GB with SMOTE at 1:5 and at 1:20, $+0.0072$, which the offset brings to $+0.0047$). The offset does not move Brier score consistently toward no correction: the gap shrinks in magnitude in 11 of 24 cells (2 of 12 for LR, 9 of 12 for GB). The offset also raises mean $|a|$ in all 24 cells ($p<0.05$ in 10; for GB with SMOTE at 1:20 only beyond the second decimal): LR at 1:20 goes from $|a|=0.80$ (reweight), 0.66 (ROS) and 0.56 (SMOTE) to 2.25, 2.48 and 2.66, against 0.75 for no correction (Table~\ref{tab:citl}). The arithmetic is that the offset shifts every logit by $\log\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}-\log\frac{\pi_{\text{eff}}}{1-\pi_{\text{eff}}}\approx -3$ at 1:20 and therefore raises $a$ by about $3$; the raw corrected LR models overestimated risk by less than one logit ($a=-0.38$ to $-0.78$), so the offset overshoots into a strong underestimate. The raw models may be this close to the deployment prevalence because with 12 positives and nearly separable classes they are not calibrated at $\pi_{\text{eff}}$ in the first place; we did not test this. We read H4 as \emph{refuted in spirit} (no repair, higher $|a|$) although the registered threshold is formally met in 23 cells.
