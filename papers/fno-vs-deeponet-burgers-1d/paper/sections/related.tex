\textbf{Neural operators.} The FNO~\cite{li2020fourier} evaluates a learned multiplier on the lowest Fourier modes of the hidden state and adds a pointwise linear skip; its Burgers experiments are the template for our data distribution. DeepONet~\cite{lu2019deeponet} builds on the universal approximation theorem for operators and has a branch/trunk decomposition whose trunk can be queried anywhere while the branch needs fixed sensors. The framework paper~\cite{kovachki2021neural} places both in a common formalism of learning maps between function spaces. Earlier, PCA-based encoder--decoder surrogates gave a reduced-basis view of the same problem~\cite{bhattacharya2020model}, which is the closest published relative of our MLP control.

\textbf{Comparisons and benchmarks.} Lu et al.~\cite{lu2021comprehensive} compare DeepONet and FNO, each with practical extensions, on 16 benchmarks and report comparable performance in relatively simple settings, with the FNO deteriorating on complex geometries and noisy data. This is the closest prior work to ours; ours is far smaller in scale and adds MLP/CNN controls and a resolution test. Our result does \emph{not} reproduce that near-parity: our plain, reimplemented DeepONet is far less accurate than our FNO (Section~\ref{sec:results}). We compare against no published error value, and we read our DeepONet numbers as a lower bound on what DeepONet can do, not as a contradiction of~\cite{lu2021comprehensive}. PDEBench~\cite{takamoto2022pdebench} supplies 1D Burgers data at much larger scale with FNO, U-Net and PINN baselines (no DeepONet), and PINO adds physics-informed losses~\cite{li2021physics}, which we do not use.

\textbf{Discretisation invariance.} Bartolucci et al.~\cite{bartolucci2023representation} argue that FNO-type layers are not exactly discretisation-invariant once aliasing and truncation are considered, and the convolutional neural operator~\cite{raoni2023convolutional} is designed to address this. Neither is reimplemented here; our test uses band-limited inputs, where the aliasing issue is absent by construction (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Numerics.} Data are generated with the ETDRK4 stiff integrator~\cite{kassam2005fourth}, and all networks are trained with Adam~\cite{kingma2014adam}.
