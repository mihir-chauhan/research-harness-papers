\textbf{H1 (capacity needed): partly supported, confounded by optimisation.} In the sweeps (Tables~\ref{tab:sv},\ref{tab:sl}), channels below 8 bits (V2\_L5, V3\_L5, V6\_L2, V6\_L3) have train accuracy at most \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l3/train_acc/mean:3} and held-out accuracy at most \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l3/heldout_acc/mean:3}. But accuracy does not switch on at 8 bits: V4\_L5 (10 bits) still has train accuracy \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l5/train_acc/mean:3}, and V6\_L4 (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l4/capacity_bits/mean:1} bits) \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l4/train_acc/mean:3}. Accuracy rises only for much larger channels (V16\_L5: \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l5/train_acc/mean:3} train). So capacity is necessary, but at this budget it is far from sufficient; ``capacity below 8 bits caps accuracy'' is true but uninformative.

\textbf{H2 (larger channel hurts generalisation): refuted in this study, with a caveat.} V16\_L8 reaches held-out accuracy \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3} (Gumbel-softmax) and \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3} (REINFORCE) while the tight V4\_L4 reaches \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} (Gumbel-softmax) and \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} (REINFORCE). The train--held-out gap is smaller at V4\_L4 (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/gen_gap/mean:3} for Gumbel-softmax) only because train accuracy is itself \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:3}: the agents have not learned the task there. Topsim is also higher, not lower, at V16\_L8 (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/topsim/mean:3}) than at V4\_L4 (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/topsim/mean:3}) for Gumbel-softmax. The over-capacity channel does carry a large gap (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/gen_gap/mean:3} and \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/gen_gap/mean:3}), and the random code shows the extreme: train accuracy \rhval{main/random-code-reference/v16_l8/train_acc/mean:3} at V16\_L8 but held-out \rhval{main/random-code-reference/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3}. The data do not tell us whether a well-trained tight channel would generalise better. The curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}) show why: Gumbel-softmax at V4\_L4 is unstable rather than slow. Train accuracy peaks early and then collapses (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/min:3}--\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/max:3} at the end of the 6000-step main runs, and at most \rhval{abl_longtrain/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/max:3} at 20000, with one or two distinct messages in the control runs of Table~\ref{tab:long}); the 300-step sanity run (train \rhval{run/b2ea4d6ad3/train_acc:3}) beat the 6000-step run of the same seed (\rhval{run/634c79c737/train_acc:3}). Longer training therefore does not help this system under $\tau=1$, lr $2\times10^{-3}$; we did not test other temperatures or learning rates, so the cause (e.g.\ a collapsing sender or temperature) is not identified.

\textbf{H3 (learned protocols are not compositional): supported.} Topsim of learned protocols lies between \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/topsim/mean:3} and \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/topsim/mean:3}, versus \rhval{main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v4_l4/topsim/mean:3} for the oracle code (Gumbel-softmax vs oracle, Welch $p=$ \rhval{cmp/main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v4_l4/topsim/welch_p}, \rhval{cmp/main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v8_l4/topsim/welch_p} and \rhval{cmp/main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v16_l8/topsim/welch_p} at V4\_L4, V8\_L4 and V16\_L8). On V4\_L4 and V8\_L4 the oracle reaches \rhval{main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v4_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} held-out accuracy against at most \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v8_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} for learned senders. However the oracle is \emph{not} perfect at V16\_L8 (held-out \rhval{main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3}): a listener GRU reading 8 positions of which 4 are constant padding generalises imperfectly, a failure of the reference listener rather than of the code that we did not investigate further. The random code has topsim near zero (at most \rhval{main/random-code-reference/v4_l4/topsim/mean:3}) and held-out accuracy \rhval{main/random-code-reference/v4_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} everywhere, as expected for an unstructured code.

\textbf{H4 (Gumbel-softmax beats REINFORCE): not supported.} On held-out accuracy the difference is within noise on every task (V16\_L8: \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3}, Welch $p=$ \rhval{cmp/main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/heldout_acc/welch_p:2}). REINFORCE has clearly higher train accuracy on V4\_L4 (\rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:3}), where Gumbel-softmax collapses; at V8\_L4 the train-accuracy difference (\rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v8_l4/train_acc/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v8_l4/train_acc/mean:3}) is within one standard deviation, and the topsim differences are within noise at V4\_L4 and V8\_L4 (Welch $p=$ \rhval{cmp/main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/topsim/welch_p:3} and \rhval{cmp/main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v8_l4/topsim/welch_p:2}); only the V16\_L8 topsim difference (REINFORCE higher, $p=$ \rhval{cmp/main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/topsim/welch_p:3}) is clear. Unlike Havrylov and Titov, who found Gumbel-softmax converging much faster, we did not observe an advantage for it. REINFORCE is still improving at 6000 steps at V4\_L4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}; train \rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:3} at 6000 steps and \rhval{abl_longtrain/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:3} at 20000), but its held-out accuracy stays at \rhval{abl_longtrain/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3} after 20000 steps (Table~\ref{tab:long}) because it memorises (train \rhval{abl_longtrain/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:2}) rather than generalises. With three seeds and learning rate, temperature and entropy weight untuned for either, we make no claim about the estimators in general.

\textbf{Failure cases.} Per-seed variability is large: the std of Gumbel-softmax held-out accuracy at V16\_L8 is \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/heldout_acc/std:3} around a mean of \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3}, so a different seed set could change the ranking with REINFORCE. All learned systems at V4\_L4 and V8\_L4 fail (held-out at most \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v8_l4/heldout_acc/mean:3}), so no statement about compositionality at tight capacity can be drawn from well-trained protocols.
