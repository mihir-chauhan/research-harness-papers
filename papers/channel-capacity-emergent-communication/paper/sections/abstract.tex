We study how vocabulary size $V$ and message length $L$ affect accuracy on held-out attribute combinations and topographic similarity in a Lewis reconstruction game over $4\times4$ attribute-value objects, using small GRU speakers and listeners trained with straight-through Gumbel-softmax or REINFORCE. This is a small CPU study (three seeds, 6000 training steps, reimplemented baselines). Under this budget the main finding is optimisation-limited rather than capacity-limited: channels at or slightly above the 8 bits needed to name the 256 objects are mostly not learned, and held-out accuracy is near zero for both estimators; the larger channel (V16\_L8, 32 bits) is the only main task where learned protocols generalise to some held-out combinations (about \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:1}--\rhval{main/reinforce-sender-reimplemented/v16_l8/heldout_acc/mean:1} held-out accuracy; in the sweeps, V16\_L5 also reaches \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l5/heldout_acc/mean:3}), the opposite of the hypothesis that a channel larger than needed hurts generalisation. Training curves show that this is not simple under-training for Gumbel-softmax: at V4\_L4 its train accuracy peaks early and then collapses to near zero (at most \rhval{abl_longtrain/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/train_acc/max:3} at 20000 steps), whereas REINFORCE is still improving at 6000 steps. Learned protocols have topographic similarity between about \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v4_l4/topsim/mean:1} and \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l5/topsim/mean:1}, far below an oracle compositional code (\rhval{main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v4_l4/topsim/mean:1}), and a fixed random code of equal capacity memorises the training objects at V8\_L4 and V16\_L8 (\rhval{main/random-code-reference/v4_l4/train_acc/mean:2} train accuracy at V4\_L4, owing to message collisions) but generalises to none. Gumbel-softmax and REINFORCE are indistinguishable on held-out accuracy with three seeds. The study does not establish how a well-trained protocol depends on capacity: Gumbel-softmax is unstable at tight channels with the untuned $\tau=1$ and learning rate $2\times10^{-3}$ used here.
