In a small controlled Lewis reconstruction game, learned protocols (Gumbel-softmax and REINFORCE) are far from compositional, with topographic similarity at most \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v16_l5/topsim/mean:3} against \rhval{main/oracle-compositional-code-reference/v4_l4/topsim/mean:3} for an oracle code, and generalise to held-out combinations only in the larger channels we tried (V16\_L8, and V16\_L5 in the sweep). Capacity below 8 bits clearly prevents learning, but capacity near the minimum is not enough with our untuned settings (Gumbel-softmax peaks early then collapses at V4\_L4; REINFORCE memorises without generalising), so the hypothesis that an over-sized channel generalises worse could not be tested fairly and was reversed in our data. The estimators were indistinguishable on held-out accuracy with three seeds. Natural next steps are tuning temperature and learning rate per system, and more seeds.
