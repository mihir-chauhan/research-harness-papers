The length sweep is non-monotonic: V6\_L6 has the highest train accuracy (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l6/train_acc/mean:3}) and V6\_L8 is lower (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l8/train_acc/mean:3}); their held-out accuracies (\rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l6/heldout_acc/mean:3} vs \rhval{main/gumbel-softmax-sender-receiver/v6_l8/heldout_acc/mean:3}) are within one standard deviation at $n=3$. Longer messages may be harder to optimise rather than limited by capacity, but we did not test this; the V4\_L4 control (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) shows that longer training alone does not rescue the Gumbel-softmax system there. Stronger channels also use more distinct messages (n\_unique\_messages in the tables), i.e.\ they drift towards one message per object, which is what the random code does by construction.
