On PhysioNet 2012 set-a, with equal-sized tuning grids and \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds of \rhval{const/n_folds}-fold patient-level cross-validation, gradient boosting on per-variable summaries (AUROC <<V main|GBDT_(summary) auroc mean 3>>) beat logistic regression (<<V main|LR_(summary) auroc mean 3>>) and all three recurrent models (<<V main|GRU-D auroc mean 3>> for GRU-D), and GRU-D did not beat a forward-filled GRU or a GRU with mask and delta inputs at the full 48 h horizon. The decay mechanism of GRU-D had no measurable effect in our ablations. Missingness-aware inputs did help the GRU at shorter horizons (\rhval{const/h12} h and \rhval{const/h24} h), and explicit count features helped logistic regression but not boosting. These conclusions are limited to one small cohort, our reimplementations, hourly binning and a modest tuning budget; they do not show that missingness-aware recurrent models are worse in general, only that on this benchmark they were not better than a tuned tree ensemble on summaries.
