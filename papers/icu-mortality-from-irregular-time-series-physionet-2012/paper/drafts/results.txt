\subsection{Main comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the primary result: the fold-averaged AUROC, AUPRC and Brier score of all five systems, mean $\pm$ standard deviation over \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds, each seed being a different \rhval{const/n_folds}-fold patient-level split. Gradient boosting on summary features is best on all three metrics (AUROC <<V main|GBDT_(summary) auroc mean 4>>). Logistic regression is second on AUROC (<<V main|LR_(summary) auroc mean 4>>), and the three recurrent models are lower and close to each other: forward-fill GRU <<V main|GRU_(forward-fill) auroc mean 4>>, mask+delta GRU <<V main|GRU_(mask+delta) auroc mean 4>>, GRU-D <<V main|GRU-D auroc mean 4>>. \subsection{Hypothesis by hypothesis}
All tests are paired over seeds (the same fold split per seed for every system), two-sided paired $t$-tests on the fold-averaged metric with $n=$\,\rhval{const/n_seeds}; they are unadjusted for multiplicity, and with five pairs per test the smallest attainable two-sided $p$ is limited, so we also report the number of seeds in which the first system wins (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).

\textbf{H1: GRU-D does not beat gradient boosting on summaries (supported).} GRU-D is lower than GBDT by <<A d_grud_gbdt_auroc 4>> AUROC (paired $p=$<<A p_grud_gbdt_auroc 4>>, GRU-D is better in <<A w_grud_gbdt_auroc 0>> of the five seeds), and by <<A d_grud_gbdt_auprc 4>> AUPRC. The registered refutation criterion (GRU-D better by more than \rhval{const/margin_auroc:2} with $p<\rhval{const/alpha:2}$) is not met; the observed difference is in the opposite direction. GRU-D is also below logistic regression on AUROC (<<A d_grud_lr_auroc 4>>, $p=$<<A p_grud_lr_auroc 4>>), although the AUPRC difference to LR (<<A d_grud_lr_auprc 4>>, $p=$<<A p_grud_lr_auprc 4>>) is not distinguishable from zero with five seeds. Gradient boosting beats LR (<<A d_gbdt_lr_auroc 4>> AUROC, $p=$<<A p_gbdt_lr_auroc 4>>), with equal Brier score (difference <<A d_gbdt_lr_brier 4>>, $p=$<<A p_gbdt_lr_brier 4>>), so its advantage is in ranking rather than in calibration.

\textbf{H2: mask and delta inputs help a GRU over forward-filling (not supported at 48 h).} The mask+delta GRU differs from the forward-fill GRU by <<A d_md_ffill_auroc 4>> AUROC ($p=$<<A p_md_ffill_auroc 4>>) and GRU-D by <<A d_grud_ffill_auroc 4>> ($p=$<<A p_grud_ffill_auroc 4>>); the AUPRC differences are <<A d_md_ffill_auprc 4>> and <<A d_grud_ffill_auprc 4>> (both $p>\rhval{const/alpha:2}$, see Table~\ref{tab:paired}). The effect is therefore indistinguishable from zero here with this number of seeds. The hypothesis is not supported at the full horizon, and holds at shorter horizons (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\textbf{H3: decay adds over mask+delta (not supported).} GRU-D minus the mask+delta GRU is <<A d_grud_md_auroc 4>> AUROC ($p=$<<A p_grud_md_auroc 4>>, GRU-D is better in <<A w_grud_md_auroc 0>> of the five seeds); the decay ablations of Section~\ref{sec:ablations} point the same way.

\textbf{Per-regime breakdown and failure case.} Table~\ref{tab:icu} splits the pooled out-of-fold AUROC by ICU type. GBDT leads in every unit and its margin is largest in the cardiac-surgery recovery unit (CSRU: <<V main|GBDT_(summary) auroc_icu2 mean 3>> against <<V main|GRU-D auroc_icu2 mean 3>> for GRU-D), the unit with the lowest mortality (overall mortality <<V data|cohort_statistics mortality mean 3>>, CSRU <<V data|cohort_statistics icu2_mortality mean 3>>) and therefore the fewest positive cases, a regime in which the neural models may be disadvantaged by having the least signal to learn from (we did not test this); the medical ICU is hardest for every system (GBDT <<V main|GBDT_(summary) auroc_icu3 mean 3>>). The one place where a recurrent model is best or tied is not systematic: in the coronary care unit the mask+delta GRU (<<V main|GRU_(mask+delta) auroc_icu1 mean 3>>) beats the forward-fill GRU (<<V main|GRU_(forward-fill) auroc_icu1 mean 3>>) by more than the seed standard deviation, but in the CSRU the ordering reverses. These per-unit numbers come from pooled predictions of about \rhval{const/n_stays} patients split four ways with a few hundred deaths in total, so they are descriptive only.

\textbf{Calibration and complexity.} The mean predicted risk of GBDT (<<V main|GBDT_(summary) mean_pred mean 3>>) and of GRU-D (<<V main|GRU-D mean_pred mean 3>>) is close to the observed mortality (<<V data|cohort_statistics mortality mean 3>>), so the Brier differences are not driven by a global bias. The recurrent models stop early (GRU-D after <<V main|GRU-D mean_epochs_or_trees mean 1>> epochs on average, against the cap of \rhval{const/max_epochs}), i.e.\ the inner-validation loss stops improving after few passes over \rhval{const/n_train_fold} training patients; we read this as an overfitting regime but did not test it with, e.g., learning curves.
