\textbf{Scale and statistics.} This is a CPU study on one open cohort of \rhval{const/n_stays} stays with \rhval{const/n_deaths} deaths. The \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds re-draw the fold split and the initialisation but reuse the same patients, so the seed-to-seed spread understates the sampling uncertainty over patients, and the paired $t$-tests with five pairs have little power and are not corrected for the many comparisons we report; differences of a few thousandths of AUROC (e.g.\ among the three recurrent models) are not resolvable. The fold-level standard deviation of AUROC within a seed (about the size reported for \texttt{auroc\_fold\_sd} in the registry) is several times larger than the seed standard deviation of the fold average.

\textbf{Tuning.} Tuning used two seeds and a hold-out from the training portion of fold~0 with grids of a handful of cells per system. The tuning patients overlap with the test folds of the evaluation seeds (different fold assignments but the same \rhval{const/n_stays} patients), so selection is mildly optimistic for every system alike. Because the hold-out contains only a few hundred patients, the selection of a grid cell is itself noisy. The recurrent models are the least thoroughly tuned (hidden size, learning rate, dropout, weight decay only; no depth, no bidirectionality, no input dropout, no learning-rate schedule, no ensembling), and larger or longer-trained recurrent models, or models pre-trained on larger ICU cohorts, may behave differently. The GBDT baseline (scikit-learn's histogram implementation) is not XGBoost/LightGBM, though these implement the same algorithm family.

\textbf{Reimplemented baselines and deviations.} All five systems are our own implementations, not the authors' code. GRU-D deviates from the original in the hourly binning (within-hour timing is lost for every model), the head over static features, and the use of the last hidden state; these may cost it relative to the published model. Static descriptors are taken as the first recorded value, and height and weight are about half and a few percent missing respectively (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Summary features are a fixed set (min, max, mean, std, first, last, count); richer engineered features (trends, time-windowed statistics) could widen the gap in favour of the summary models.

\textbf{Scope.} One dataset (set-a only; sets b and c and external validation, e.g.\ on other hospitals, are not used), one outcome and no calibration post-processing. No claims are made about newer irregular-sampling architectures~\cite{shukla2019interpolation,horn2019set,rubanova2019latent,zhang2021graph,tipirneni2021self}, which were not run. The explanations offered for observed patterns (overfitting of recurrent models on a small cohort, redundancy of counts for trees, usefulness of masks when little is observed) are hypotheses; only the decay and count ablations and the horizon sweep isolate any factor, and none isolates the overfitting explanation. A full study would add more seeds and bootstrap confidence intervals over patients, nested cross-validation for tuning, MIMIC-III/eICU-scale data and external validation.
