Recurrent networks with explicit missingness handling, such as GRU-D, are a standard reference for irregularly sampled ICU data, but strong feature-based baselines are often reported to be competitive. We run a small controlled comparison on the open PhysioNet 2012 set-a cohort (\rhval{const/n_stays} stays): logistic regression and gradient boosting on per-variable summary statistics against a forward-filled GRU, a GRU with mask and time-delta inputs, and GRU-D, all reimplemented, with grids of similar size tuned on hold-out data of separate tuning seeds, and evaluated with \rhval{const/n_seeds} seeds of \rhval{const/n_folds}-fold patient-level cross-validation (AUROC, AUPRC, Brier). Gradient boosting was best (AUROC <<V main|GBDT_(summary) auroc mean 3>>), ahead of logistic regression (<<V main|LR_(summary) auroc mean 3>>) and of GRU-D (<<V main|GRU-D auroc mean 3>>); GRU-D was in the same range as a forward-filled GRU (<<V main|GRU_(forward-fill) auroc mean 3>>) and a mask+delta GRU (<<V main|GRU_(mask+delta) auroc mean 3>>), and removing its input or hidden decay changed AUROC by less than about a thousandth. Mask and delta inputs did help the GRU when only the first \rhval{const/h12} or \rhval{const/h24} hours were used, and count features helped logistic regression but not boosting. The result is negative for the recurrent models on this cohort; it is limited to one small dataset, reimplemented baselines and a modest tuning budget.
