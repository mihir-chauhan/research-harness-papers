# Landscape

**Closest work.** Ovadia et al. (ovadia2019can) benchmarked predictive uncertainty (single network, temperature scaling, MC dropout, deep ensembles, and others) across shift intensities on corrupted CIFAR-10/ImageNet and found that quality degrades with shift and that deep ensembles were the most robust of the methods compared; post-hoc calibration on in-distribution data helped in-distribution but much less under shift. Guo et al. (guo2017calibration) introduced temperature scaling (TS) and the ECE/NLL protocol; Tomani et al. (tomani2020post) show that post-hoc calibration fitted in-domain breaks under domain drift and propose perturbing the calibration set. Deep ensembles (lakshminarayanan2016simple) and MC dropout (gal2015dropout) are the standard non-post-hoc baselines; the validity of MC dropout as a Bayesian posterior is questioned by Le Folgoc et al. (folgoc2021is). Hendrycks and Dietterich (hendrycks2019benchmarking) supply the corruption-benchmark framing; Uncertainty Baselines (nado2021uncertainty) is the tuned GPU-scale implementation suite; Laplace Redux (daxberger2021laplace), SWAG (maddox2019simple) and neural ensemble search (zaidi2020neural) are further approaches out of scope here.

**Gap.** Those results are GPU-scale on image corruptions. A tiny, fully controllable setting (scikit-learn digits, an MLP, two synthetic shift families with a continuous intensity knob, 5 seeds) lets one (i) run the same comparison on a CPU in minutes, (ii) separate rotation from additive noise as shift families, and (iii) isolate the mechanism behind TS failure with a controlled "oracle" ablation where T is fitted on shifted validation data. This is a small replication/ablation study, not a new method.
