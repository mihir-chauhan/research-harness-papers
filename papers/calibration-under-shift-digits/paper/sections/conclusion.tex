On digits with rotation and noise shift, a temperature fitted on in-distribution validation data did not stay calibrated: TS ECE rose from 0.016 in distribution to 0.803 at r60 and 0.618 at n1.0 (about 50 and 39 times), although TS still lowered NLL relative to the uncalibrated MLP at all shifted conditions and its ECE gain over the MLP shrank to 3\% at r60. A temperature fitted on shift-matched validation data brought ECE to at most 0.092 in all conditions, so the failure is a temperature mismatch for ECE, but it did so partly by producing near-uniform predictions. Contrary to our pre-registered expectation and to the literature we cite, MC dropout, not the 5-member ensemble, had the lowest ECE, NLL and Brier in this setting, in part (per an exploratory decomposition) because of the regularisation of the dropout-trained network. The in-distribution benefit of TS was not statistically supported at 5 seeds. These findings are limited to a small MLP on a small dataset and a handful of seeds.
