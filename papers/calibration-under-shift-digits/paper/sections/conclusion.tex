On digits with rotation and noise shift, a temperature fitted on in-distribution validation data did not stay calibrated: TS ECE rose from \rhval{main/mlp-+-temperature-scaling/rot0/ece/mean:3} in distribution to \rhval{main/mlp-+-temperature-scaling/rot60/ece/mean:3} at r60 and \rhval{main/mlp-+-temperature-scaling/noise1.0/ece/mean:3} at n1.0, although TS still lowered NLL relative to the uncalibrated MLP at all shifted conditions and its relative ECE change against the MLP shrank to \rhval{cmp/abl_oracle/mlp/rot60/ece/rel_delta:pct0}\% at r60. A temperature fitted on shift-matched validation data brought ECE to at most \rhval{abl_oracle/ts-oracle-shifted-val/rot30/ece/mean:3} in all conditions, so the failure is a temperature mismatch for ECE, but it did so partly by producing near-uniform predictions. Contrary to our pre-registered expectation and to the literature we cite, MC dropout, not the 5-member ensemble, had the lowest ECE, NLL and Brier in this setting, in part (per an exploratory decomposition) because of the regularisation of the dropout-trained network. The in-distribution benefit of TS was not statistically supported at 5 seeds. These findings are limited to a small MLP on a small dataset and a handful of seeds.
