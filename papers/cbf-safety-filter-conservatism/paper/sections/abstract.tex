A control barrier function (CBF) safety filter is usually designed in continuous time but executed at a fixed sampling period. In a small CPU study (numpy, closed-form single-constraint filters, 5 seeds of 100 randomised scenarios) we measure how the class-K gain $\alpha$ and the period $\Delta t$ affect safety violations and conservatism for a double integrator and a unicycle among three circular obstacles. We compare a continuous-time CBF applied at discrete steps, a discrete-time CBF condition on the zero-order-hold successor, and a distance-threshold braking heuristic, all reimplemented, over a $5\times5$ grid of $\alpha$ from 0.5 to 10 and $\Delta t$ from 0.01 to 0.2\,s. On the double integrator the continuous-time CBF was safe unless $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$ were both large (violation rate up to 0.386), with one small-gain exception (0.006 at $\alpha=0.5,\Delta t=0.2$); one-factor sweeps miss both. The discrete-time condition was nearly safe at the default (0.008), but its violation rate rose with $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$, with a mean worst penetration of at most 0.016\,m where $\alpha\Delta t<1$ and up to 0.679\,m, comparable to the unfiltered controller, where $\alpha\Delta t\ge1$. These violations occur between control samples (at-sample rate at most 0.002) and are absent when $\Delta t$ equals the integration step (probed for $\alpha\ge2$). On the unicycle an axle-level metric with a 0.15\,m buffer showed no violations; at barrier level the discrete-time filter violated in 0.464 of episodes at the default, at the samples too. The discrete-time filter had a lower violation rate than the continuous-time one in only one of the 50 grid cells, but on the double integrator this comparison is confounded by design order: our continuous-time filter is second-order and velocity-aware, our discrete-time filter first-order on position. The heuristic violated at its default threshold (0.110). Larger $\alpha$ shortened the time to goal on the double integrator but not beyond $\alpha=2$ on the unicycle. The results are specific to our scenarios, filters and obstacle-selection rule and are not a general ranking.
