\textbf{Scenarios.} Each seed generates 100 episodes (start, goal, three obstacles of radius 0.5--0.8 placed near the start--goal line); all systems see identical episodes. Horizon 30\,s, goal tolerance 0.15\,m. Seeds 0--4. Default setting (not tuned): $\alpha=2$, $\Delta t=0.05$\,s, $d_{th}=1$\,m. Goal arrival is tested at control instants, so the time to goal has resolution $\Delta t$.

\textbf{Metrics.} \emph{violation\_rate}: fraction of episodes whose clearance (distance to the obstacle boundary, checked every 0.005\,s, hence also between samples) was ever below $-10^{-9}$\,m (the same tolerance for every system; a point projected exactly onto the boundary sits at about $-10^{-14}$\,m by round-off and is not counted); \emph{success\_rate}; \emph{time\_to\_goal} (30\,s if not reached); \emph{min\_clearance} (mean per-episode minimum clearance); \emph{worst\_penetration} (deepest violation within a seed's 100 episodes). On the double integrator this clearance is the barrier value. On the unicycle the barrier constrains the look-ahead point against radii inflated by $L$, whereas the clearance above is measured at the axle against the raw radius, so it has a buffer of about 0.13--0.15\,m. We therefore also report \emph{barrier\_violation\_rate} and \emph{barrier\_penetration} (look-ahead point against the inflated radius; identical to the metrics above for the double integrator) and \emph{sample\_violation\_rate} (the barrier-level check applied only at control sample instants, same tolerance). Barrier-level metrics are the primary safety metrics.

\textbf{Hypotheses and tests (as stated in the proposal).} H1: the violation rate of CT-CBF grows with $\Delta t$ and with $\alpha$ and is about zero when both are small (test: its one-factor sweeps; refuted if violations do not grow). H2: at the same $\alpha$ and large $\Delta t$, DT-CBF has a lower violation rate than CT-CBF, at the price of a longer time to goal (period sweep; refuted if the DT-CBF rate is not lower). H3: time to goal and minimum clearance decrease monotonically with $\alpha$ for the CBF filters (gain sweep; refuted if time to goal does not decrease). H4: the braking heuristic has a non-zero violation rate on the double integrator at the default setting while the CBF filters have none (main table; refuted if the heuristic's rate is zero or a CBF filter violates).

\textbf{Designs.} The systems are abbreviated Nominal (unfiltered), Braking, CT-CBF and DT-CBF. The main table uses the default setting. The effect of $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$ is studied on a full $5\times5$ grid, $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2,5,10\}$ by $\Delta t\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$\,s, for CT-CBF and DT-CBF on both plants (500 runs); the one-factor sweeps shown below are slices of this grid ($\Delta t=0.05$, respectively $\alpha=2$). Further groups vary the braking threshold and the obstacle-selection rule, and a probe at $\Delta t=0.005$\,s (equal to the plant step, so no between-sample check) for $\alpha\in\{2,5,10\}$ on both plants (60 runs); 660 runs in total.

\textbf{Compute.} CPU only, numpy; a run typically takes under one second (a few seconds at most on the shared machine). Table~\ref{tab:main} gives means $\pm$ sample std (ddof=1) over 5 seeds (each a mean over 100 episodes); sweep tables give means over seeds and figures show mean $\pm$ sample std. Baselines are our reimplementations, not the authors' code.
