\textbf{CBF filters.} CBF quadratic programs filter a nominal controller~\cite{ames2017control}; \cite{ames2019control} survey the theory. For position constraints on a double integrator (relative degree two) we use the high-order construction~\cite{xiao2019control}.
\textbf{Discrete time and sampling.} Discrete-time CBF conditions appear within model predictive control~\cite{zeng2021safety} and have high-order and adaptive extensions~\cite{xiong2023discrete}; \cite{niu2021safety} study CBF synthesis for sampled-data systems with unknown dynamics. Breeden et al.~\cite{breeden2021control} give sampled-data forward-invariance conditions (two derived from continuous-time and one from discrete-time barrier functions) and compare their conservativeness; Taylor et al.~\cite{taylor2022safety} synthesise sampled-data CBF controllers from approximate discrete-time models; Bahati et al.~\cite{bahati2023sample} take an input-to-state-safety view of sample-and-hold control and modulate the sampled input so that the original safe set is not violated. Our study is not a new condition: it measures how a simple discrete-time condition and a continuous-time one behave under the same sampling in simulation.
\textbf{Alternatives.} Predictive safety filters certify safety over a horizon~\cite{wabersich2021predictive}; CBFs have been compared with artificial potential fields for obstacle avoidance, analytically and with supporting experiments~\cite{singletary2021comparative}; our comparison against a braking heuristic is in that spirit but is purely empirical.
