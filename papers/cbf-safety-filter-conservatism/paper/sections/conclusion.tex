In this small simulation study the continuous-time CBF applied at discrete steps was safe on the double integrator except when $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$ were both large (violation rate up to 0.386) and in one small-gain, large-period cell (0.006), which one-factor sweeps through the default miss. Our first-order discrete-time condition was nearly safe at the default (0.008), but its violation rate rose with $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$ up to 1.000 where $\alpha\Delta t\ge1$ (mean worst penetration up to 0.679\,m), and it had a lower rate than our second-order continuous-time filter in one grid cell only. On the double integrator its violations occur between control samples (at-sample rate at most 0.002); on the unicycle they are present at the samples, and an axle-level metric hid them (0.464 at the default at barrier level). For CT-CBF the obstacle-selection rule changed safety more than $\Delta t$ did at the default gain. These results are specific to our implementations and scenarios.
