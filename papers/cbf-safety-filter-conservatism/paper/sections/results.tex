Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the main comparison. The unfiltered nominal controller violates in every episode on both plants (violation rate 1.000). On the double integrator, CT-CBF has violation rate 0.000, DT-CBF 0.008 and the braking heuristic 0.110; time to goal is 7.800 (CT), 7.542 (DT) and 7.480 (braking) seconds against 5.170 for the nominal controller. DT-CBF is faster than CT-CBF (Welch $p=0.007$ on time to goal).

\textbf{Unicycle: the axle metric hides violations.} Measured at the axle, all three filters have zero violations on the unicycle. At barrier level (Table~\ref{tab:main}) CT-CBF and the braking heuristic remain at 0.000 but DT-CBF violates in 0.464 of the episodes at the default setting, with a mean worst penetration of 0.007\,m. These violations are present at the control instants (Table~\ref{tab:samp}; Sec.~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\textbf{Joint dependence on $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$.} Table~\ref{tab:grid} and Fig.~\ref{fig:grid} give, for every cell of the grid, the barrier-level violation rate, the mean over seeds of the worst penetration and the mean minimum clearance. CT-CBF is exactly safe on both one-factor slices through the default (Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}), but the slices miss a corner. On the double integrator CT-CBF violates when both are large (0.270 at $\alpha=10,\Delta t=0.1$; 0.386 at $\alpha=10,\Delta t=0.2$, mean worst penetration 0.119\,m; 0.020 at $\alpha=5,\Delta t=0.2$) and, less expectedly, at small gain with the largest period (0.006 at $\alpha=0.5,\Delta t=0.2$, 3 of 500 episodes, mean worst penetration 0.073\,m). The other 21 double-integrator CT-CBF cells are at zero. On the unicycle CT-CBF is at zero except at $\Delta t=0.2$ with large gain (0.862 at $\alpha=5$, 1.000 at $\alpha=10$, mean worst penetration 0.010 and 0.093\,m), a violation that the axle metric does not show.

DT-CBF on the double integrator is at zero for $\alpha\le1$, does not decrease with $\alpha$ at fixed $\Delta t$ or with $\Delta t$ at fixed $\alpha$ (0.008, 0.018, 0.070 at $\alpha=2$ for $\Delta t=0.05,0.1,0.2$) and reaches 1.000 in the three cells where $\gamma=\min(1,\alpha\Delta t)$ saturates ($\alpha\Delta t\ge1$). Where $\alpha\Delta t<1$ the mean worst penetration is at most 0.016\,m (at $\alpha=2,\Delta t=0.2$; 0.013\,m at $\alpha=5,\Delta t=0.1$; 0.003\,m or less in the other cells, although the rate reaches 0.618 at $\alpha=10,\Delta t=0.05$). Where it saturates the penetrations are comparable to the unfiltered controller: mean worst penetration 0.109\,m at $\alpha=10,\Delta t=0.1$ and 0.679\,m at $\alpha=5$ and $10$ with $\Delta t=0.2$, mean minimum clearance $-0.05$ and $-0.18$\,m, against 0.759\,m for the nominal controller in Table~\ref{tab:main}. On the unicycle DT-CBF violates at barrier level in every cell with $\alpha\ge2$, with a mean worst penetration of at most 0.043\,m over the whole grid.

\textbf{Hypotheses.} Each verdict uses the test stated in the proposal (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}); grid observations beyond it are post hoc.
H1 is refuted on its test: on both one-factor sweeps through the default CT-CBF is at 0.00 on both plants. It holds only in a weaker, post-hoc form: in the full grid CT-CBF violations appear when $\alpha$ and $\Delta t$ are both large, plus one small-gain, large-period cell on the double integrator, and the dependence is not monotone (0.006 at $\alpha=0.5$ against 0.000 at $\alpha=1,2$ for $\Delta t=0.2$). We call it partly supported.
H2 is refuted on both plants. On the period sweep at $\alpha=2$ the DT-CBF rate at large $\Delta t$ is above that of CT-CBF (double integrator 0.018 and 0.070 against 0.000 at $\Delta t=0.1$ and $0.2$; unicycle, barrier level, 0.734 and 0.970 against 0.000), and DT-CBF is faster, not slower (7.542 versus 7.800\,s at the default; Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}). Over the grid DT-CBF is lower than CT-CBF in one double-integrator cell ($\alpha=0.5,\Delta t=0.2$, 0.000 against 0.006) and in no unicycle cell. This verdict is confounded by design order on the double integrator: CT-CBF is a second-order, velocity-aware condition and our DT-CBF a first-order condition on position; a high-order DT-CBF~\cite{xiong2023discrete} was not tried, so the verdict concerns these two implementations only.
H3 is partly supported: on the double integrator time to goal and clearance fall with $\alpha$ for both filters (CT-CBF 10.93 to 7.05\,s and 0.95 to 0.01\,m from $\alpha=0.5$ to 10; Table~\ref{tab:alpha}); on the unicycle the test fails, since the time to goal falls from $\alpha=0.5$ to 2 (12.84 to 11.86\,s for CT-CBF) and is then slightly larger (11.88, 11.92\,s), and clearance flattens at 0.14\,m.
H4 fails its test as written, because a CBF filter violates at the default: the heuristic violates on the double integrator (0.110) and CT-CBF does not, but DT-CBF has a small non-zero rate (0.008, not distinguishable from CT-CBF at 5 seeds). We call it partly supported (it holds for CT-CBF).
