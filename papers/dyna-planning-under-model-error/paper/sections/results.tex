We go hypothesis by hypothesis (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Table~\ref{tab:sweepn}, Figs.~\ref{fig:sweepn}--\ref{fig:curveshort}).

\textbf{H1 (planning helps with a correct model): supported.} On the static maze Dyna-Q at $n=10$ reaches the goal \rhval{main/dyna-q/static/cum_reward/mean:1} times against \rhval{main/q-learning/static/cum_reward/mean:1} for Q-learning (paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/q-learning/static/cum_reward/paired_p:sci1}$), and the early reward is higher (\rhval{main/dyna-q/static/early_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/q-learning/static/early_reward/mean:1}, paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/q-learning/static/early_reward/paired_p:3}$, \texttt{compare\_main\_early\_reward.csv}, but with a standard deviation larger than the mean, so the gap over the first \rhval{const/reward_window_steps} steps is only weakly resolved). Cumulative reward keeps rising with $n$ up to 100 for Dyna-Q on this task (Table~\ref{tab:sweepn}). Q-learning is also far below the planners on the blocking and shortcut mazes.

\textbf{H2 (stale model): half supported, half refuted.} The predicted decline of Dyna-Q's post-change reward with $n$ is \emph{not} observed on the blocking maze: it is lowest at $n=1$ and non-monotone afterwards, between \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=20/blocking/post_reward/mean:1} and \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=50/blocking/post_reward/mean:1} for $n\ge5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweepn}; 10 seeds, large variance). At small $n$ the agent is simply slow, which masks any staleness effect; at the $n$ we tried the model-induced damage is therefore not separable from sampling noise. A failure case is visible in the per-seed spread: Dyna-Q's blocking cumulative reward has a standard deviation of \rhval{main/dyna-q/blocking/cum_reward/std:1} around a mean of \rhval{main/dyna-q/blocking/cum_reward/mean:1}, with some seeds reaching the goal rarely after the wall moves. Dyna-Q+ does recover better: post-change reward \rhval{main/dyna-q+/blocking/post_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/blocking/post_reward/mean:1} for Dyna-Q at $n=10$ (paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/dyna-q+/blocking/post_reward/paired_p:3}$, uncorrected), and its advantage grows with $n$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweepn}). Fig.~\ref{fig:curveblock} shows the Dyna-Q+ mean curve rising more steeply after the change than Dyna-Q's.

\textbf{H3 (shortcut): supported.} Dyna-Q+ obtains \rhval{main/dyna-q+/shortcut/post_reward/mean:1} post-change goals versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/shortcut/post_reward/mean:1} for Dyna-Q (paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/dyna-q+/shortcut/post_reward/paired_p:sci1}$), and Dyna-Q's value is nearly constant over seeds and $n\ge5$, consistent with it never discovering the new gap (Fig.~\ref{fig:curveshort}); we did not log which path each agent used, so this explanation is an inference from the reward counts. Before the change Dyna-Q+ is not ahead of Dyna-Q (registry pre-change means are close, with Dyna-Q's spread much larger), so its gain is post-change.

\textbf{H4 (stochastic): supported in that planning's benefit shrinks with $n$; not shown to be harmful.} In Table~\ref{tab:sweepn}, Dyna-Q's mean cumulative reward on the stochastic maze falls from \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=1/stochastic/cum_reward/mean:1} at $n=1$ to \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=100/stochastic/cum_reward/mean:1} at $n=100$. At $n=1$ it beats Q-learning's \rhval{sweep_n/q-learning/stochastic/cum_reward/mean:1} on the same seeds (paired $p=\rhval{cmp/h4_n1/dyna-q/stochastic/cum_reward/paired_p:3}$); at $n\ge50$ the means (\rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=50/stochastic/cum_reward/mean:1}, \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=100/stochastic/cum_reward/mean:1}) are below Q-learning's but the gaps are within one standard deviation (\rhval{sweep_n/q-learning/stochastic/cum_reward/std:1} for Q-learning; paired $p=\rhval{cmp/h4_n50/dyna-q/stochastic/cum_reward/paired_p:2}$ and $\rhval{cmp/h4_n100/dyna-q/stochastic/cum_reward/paired_p:2}$, 10 seeds, \texttt{rh compare} on the same runs), so we cannot say planning is worse than no planning, only that its advantage is gone. Dyna-Q+ and prioritized sweeping show the same decline in means. On the stochastic-blocking maze, both errors combine and all methods earn little (Table~\ref{tab:main}); Dyna-Q+ is best there, but its absolute reward is small.

\textbf{H5 (prioritized sweeping): supported only in the sense of no detectable difference.} At $n=10$ PS and Dyna-Q are not distinguishable on cumulative reward (static \rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/static/cum_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/static/cum_reward/mean:1}, paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/prioritized-sweeping/static/cum_reward/paired_p:2}$; blocking \rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/blocking/cum_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/blocking/cum_reward/mean:1}, $p=\rhval{cmp/main/prioritized-sweeping/blocking/cum_reward/paired_p:2}$; shortcut $p=\rhval{cmp/main/prioritized-sweeping/shortcut/cum_reward/paired_p:2}$) or on post-change reward on blocking (\rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/blocking/post_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/blocking/post_reward/mean:1}). With 20 seeds and standard deviations between \rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/blocking/cum_reward/std:1} and \rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/static/cum_reward/std:1} goals on these tasks, only large effects are detectable, so this is not evidence of equivalence. PS has the best early reward on three tasks (\rhval{main/prioritized-sweeping/static/early_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{main/dyna-q/static/early_reward/mean:1} for Dyna-Q on blocking and static, paired $p=\rhval{cmp/main/prioritized-sweeping/static/early_reward/paired_p:3}$), i.e.\ it is faster at the very start, so ``indistinguishable'' holds for cumulative and post-change reward only; its dependence on $n$ is flatter than Dyna-Q's in Table~\ref{tab:sweepn}, which we conjecture is because its priority queue empties (we did not log queue length); it also shares Dyna-Q's failure to find the shortcut.

\textbf{Where the method fails.} Q-learning, Dyna-Q and PS are all poor on the stochastic-blocking maze; Dyna-Q at large $n$ on the stochastic maze has lost its advantage over Q-learning (the means are lower but not significantly so); and Dyna-Q+ at $n=1$ is worse than Dyna-Q on the static maze (\rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q+@n=1/static/cum_reward/mean:1} versus \rhval{sweep_n/dyna-q@n=1/static/cum_reward/mean:1}); we conjecture, without a control, that bonus-inflated planning values mislead a model with few samples.
