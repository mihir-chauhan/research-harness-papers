This is a small CPU study: one task (CartPole, with a numpy re-implementation of the gymnasium dynamics), one learner (CEM on a 49-parameter MLP), 5 seeds, and baselines reimplemented by us rather than taken from the cited papers. The task saturates at the 500-step cap for all nominal evaluations, so it cannot reveal a nominal cost that shows up in efficiency or tracking error. Evaluation shifts only reach $w=2$, so the widest-trained policies are not tested out of range. Parameters are randomised independently with a log-uniform range of a single shared width; correlated or per-parameter ranges, observation noise, partial observability and policies with memory or adaptation were not studied. Pendulum and locomotion tasks, larger networks and a full-scale study with more seeds, harder tasks and real hardware would be needed before any recommendation for practical range selection. The evaluation levels and sweep range were adapted after informal smoke runs that included a reported seed. The gate comparison has low statistical power and no equivalence test was performed.
