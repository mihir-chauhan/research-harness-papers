\textbf{Evaluation.} Each trained policy is evaluated on 300 episodes per level on an RNG stream disjoint from training: nominal dynamics and shifts with half-width $w\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2\}$. We report \texttt{ret\_nominal}; \texttt{ret\_w50} and \texttt{ret\_w100} (shift levels $0.5$ and $1.0$); \texttt{ret\_ood}, the mean at $w=1.5$ and $2$; and \texttt{ret\_robust}, the mean over the six shift levels (the primary metric). Levels up to $w=1$ are in-range for wide DR and the curriculum and out-of-range for narrow DR and no DR; $w=1.5,2$ are out-of-range for every system in the main table. Results are mean $\pm$ standard deviation over seeds 0--4.

\textbf{Protocol.} Main group: four systems $\times$ 5 seeds. Ablation group: curriculum gate $\rho\in\{0,0.5,0.95\}$ (0.8 is the main method). Sweep: uniform DR with $w\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,1.5,2,3\}$, 5 seeds each. Significance uses paired tests across seeds (\texttt{rh compare}). Hyperparameters were not tuned; before the reported runs, informal smoke runs (not recorded in the run registry) that included seed 1 were used to see that the evaluation levels were hard enough to separate systems, which led us to add levels $w=1.5,2$ and sweep widths up to 3. The evaluation design was thus adapted after seeing one reported seed.

\textbf{Scale.} Everything runs on CPU in seconds per run (about 70 runs). All baselines are reimplemented here. This is a small study (one task, 5 seeds, one learner); see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
