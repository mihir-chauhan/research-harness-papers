This is a small CPU study. (i) One architecture (a 128-unit MLP), one 100-step ancestral sampler, one 2D layout with four classes, three modes per class and fixed weights; the effect of dimension, number of modes or other samplers (e.g.\ DDIM \cite{song2020denoising}) is not tested, and we cannot say how results transfer to images. (ii) Five seeds, each with its own trained network; the tests are paired across seeds, uncorrected for multiplicity, and the very large effect sizes mostly reflect the near-determinism of a toy problem, so they should not be read as the effect on real data. H5 is the only borderline test ($p=0.020$) and does not survive a Bonferroni correction over the six tests (threshold 0.0083). (iii) The temperature baseline is one definition (scaling initial and injected noise); a tempered-density sampler could behave differently. The baselines are reimplemented by us and share the trained network, which isolates sampling but does not cover a network trained for temperature. (iv) Metrics rely on a $3\sigma$ radius and on a half-expected-share threshold for mode coverage; std\_ratio is computed on in-support samples only and is biased by truncation (real data give 0.975), so it understates the spread of the whole sample at large $w$. (v) The $x_0$ clipping, cosine schedule and training length were fixed and not varied; clipping may interact with large $w$. (vi) The matched-spread comparison and the interval study are exploratory and use few grid points. (vii) We did not test remedies for CFG (e.g.\ the Gibbs-like correction of \cite{moufad2025conditional}), only the interval variant. A full study would add images or a higher-dimensional benchmark with feature-space precision/recall, more seeds, and more layouts.
