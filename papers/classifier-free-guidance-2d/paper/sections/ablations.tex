\textbf{Label dropout and guidance interval.} Table~\ref{tab:abl} varies the unconditional-label probability $p$ and the noise range where guidance is applied, at $w=3$ on \texttt{mix\_overlap}. Label dropout has little effect on mode loss or accuracy (mode\_tv 0.129, 0.129, 0.128 for $p=0.1,0.02,0.3$), but $p=0.02$ gives a larger std\_ratio (0.893) and more off-support mass (0.012 vs 0.006): this is a single comparison, three values of $p$. The interval changes the picture strongly. Guidance only at low noise ($t/T\in[0,0.5]$) keeps class accuracy at 0.999 and restores mode coverage to 1.000 (mode\_tv 0.035), and swd falls from 0.783 to 0.435; guidance only at high noise ($[0.5,1]$) gives class accuracy of only 0.936 and still loses mass (mode\_tv 0.108); a middle interval ($[0.2,0.6]$) is in between (0.977, 0.060). So in this setup the class-accuracy benefit comes from the low-noise half of the chain and a large part of the mode loss from the high-noise half, which agrees in direction with the report that guidance is harmful at high noise \cite{kynknniemi2024applying}. It disagrees with their finding that guidance is largely unnecessary at low noise and beneficial in the middle: here the low-noise half carries the accuracy gain and the middle interval is worse (0.977). These are three hand-picked intervals at one $w$, with no sweep over $w$ jointly, so we do not claim the best interval or a general rule.

\textbf{Per-mode mass.} Table~\ref{tab:permode} divides each mode's share of in-support samples by its true weight (same networks and samplers as the main runs). Both CFG and low temperature move mass from minor to major modes; the inflation of the major mode (1.35 at $\tau=0.5$, 1.36 at $w=8$) is the same size for the two samplers in this table, while the minor mode is lower under temperature (0.44) than under CFG $w=3$ (0.63) and between CFG $w=3$ and $w=8$ (0.34). This is a mechanistic description of \emph{which} modes are lost; why mass moves is not isolated by an experiment here (a natural explanation is the push towards high-density regions of the conditional, but we did not test it).
